The withdrawal shifted the immediate focus from the west coast to defending Aden and neighbouring governorates. The withdrawal followed the launch of Operation Fajr al-Yemen, announced by Presidential Leadership Council chairman Rashad al-Alimi early the previous month. The operation was intended to restore state institutions, retake Sanaa and end Houthi control, but no ground advances had followed its announcement. The Houthis had meanwhile made further gains in Taiz and Lahij.

Battlefield choices and the widening displacement

Yemeni political analyst Abdulsattar al-Shamiri described the pullback as battlefield management after failures to coordinate units, supply food and ammunition, and prevent Houthi forces from outflanking government troops. He said forces around Dhubab may have feared encirclement and chosen to concentrate on defending Lahij and Aden. He considered a Houthi push toward Aden possible but difficult.

Analyst Mansour al-Muntasir said poor planning and divisions among government and southern forces had helped the Houthi advance. He cautioned that the withdrawal’s reasons were not clear-cut, while warning that Houthi control of strategic areas could strengthen their position near Bab al-Mandab and leave an advance toward Aden open.

The fighting has also displaced civilians. The International Organization for Migration recorded 347,321 people displaced from 1 August to 14 November, including 18,780 people in the week of 8–14 November. The UN refugee agency warned that nearly 9,600 displaced people faced growing risks as fighting approached Kharaz Refugee Camp in Lahij.

The United States has lost the measles-elimination status it held for 26 years after the Pan American Health Organization determined that the same strain had circulated continuously for more than a year, recognizing that measles transmission is again established in the country.

PAHO announced the decision on Tuesday. About 7,700 people in the United States were infected and six died from measles since 2025, according to figures reported by Agencia Brasil; most cases were among unvaccinated people. Kennedy Jr. said he continues to recommend MMR vaccination.

CDC director Erica Schwartz said the agency has identified the areas experiencing spread and committed $100 million to state and local health departments so they can engage with those communities. PAHO says the United States must submit a corrective action plan and then demonstrate that measles has been absent for 12 months to regain the designation; the plan is expected in the coming weeks.

What happened

Indonesia’s Constitutional Court has given lawmakers two years to amend the 1997 Military Justice Law or face its invalidation, challenging a system that places soldiers accused of ordinary crimes and human-rights violations under military jurisdiction. In its October 15 ruling, the court addressed Article 9, which requires active-duty Indonesian National Armed Forces personnel to be tried exclusively in military courts, even for ordinary criminal offenses. Human-rights groups welcomed the ruling, citing failures to investigate and prosecute serious abuses and concerns that military judges are serving soldiers, with cases handled within the chain of command and behind closed doors. The petition was brought in part by Eva Meliani Pasaribu, daughter of journalist Rico Sempurna Pasaribu, who was killed in June 2024 after reporting on a gambling operation allegedly involving a senior military official. The military’s closed-door investigation dismissed the allegation for lack of evidence.

The ruling’s implications were contested during the case. Defense Ministry official Marshal H. Haris Haryanto argued that military courts understand how criminal conduct can affect military solidarity and readiness. Armed Forces commander General Agus Subiyanto defended military judges’ independence and said commanders have a duty to ensure due process. Legal experts countered that the law needs reform to meet democratic and human-rights standards and address concerns about jurisdiction, independence and accountability.

Indonesian rights group KontraS said the decision closes a loophole it believes has shielded soldiers from accountability; it also noted that one of its employees had been attacked with acid by a group of soldiers. Brazil spends R$14.5 billion per year on the war on drugs
global_south_and_exploitedeconomy_and_businesspolitics_and_powermaterial_harmregional expansion
What happened

Brazil’s criminal-justice system spent an estimated R$14.5 billion in 2024 enforcing the Drug Law, a Cesec study finds, with prisons and overt policing accounting for nearly half; researcher Rachel Machado says there is no evidence this approach reduces drug circulation.

Released on November 17, 2026, the study counts costs for police, courts, prosecutors, public defenders, prisons and other justice bodies involved in tackling drug trafficking and prosecuting people connected to the trade. It says the total is an underestimate because it excludes some related expenses, including police operations and incursions.

The largest item was imprisoning people processed under drug law: R$3.88 billion, or 27% of the total. Military police and the Federal Highway Police spent R$3.24 billion on overt policing, 22%. Investigative police accounted for R$2.64 billion, or 18%. Another R$3.3 billion went to legal proceedings, including R$2.38 billion for courts, R$737.78 million for prosecutors and R$207.2 million for public defenders. On average, Brazil’s states devoted 7.9% of their criminal-justice budgets to enforcing the law; the federal government spent 10.9% of its budget on addressing drugs.

Machado argues that the biggest costs fall on the system’s repressive and punitive ends, while the policy fails to effectively combat criminal organizations. She also says police operations disrupt children’s access to health care and school and harm local economies. The study presents alternatives for the R$14.5 billion, including a hypothetical 6,000 Psychosocial Care Centers for Alcohol and Drugs, support for 1.8 million Bolsa Família families, or 2,536 creches; these are comparisons, not announced spending plans.

The Ministry of Justice and Public Security defended a broader approach combining enforcement with prevention, care and social development. It said federal operations in 2024 seized 779.5 tonnes of drugs and 5,243 weapons, arrested 51,649 people and caused estimated losses of R$13 billion to criminal organizations.

The design addresses a trade-off in autoregressive speech synthesis.
