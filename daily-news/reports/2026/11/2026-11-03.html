Unesco: 189 journalists killed worldwide in 2024 and 2025

What happened

UNESCO has recorded 189 journalist deaths worldwide across 2024 and 2025, with conflict zones accounting for most cases. The figures point to lethal risks for media workers covering wars, politics, crime and corruption, while few killings result in judicial resolution.

The survey, released on Monday 2, describes the total as the organization’s highest for a two-year period since 2014–2015, when it counted 215 deaths. It represents a 24% rise from the previous biennium. Of the latest cases, 127 occurred in countries affected by conflict, equal to 67% of the total, and 50 involved drones.

Palestine had the largest recorded toll, with 62 deaths—roughly one-third of all cases. In conflict settings, at least 36 journalists died during attacks on newsrooms or other facilities where media workers were present; nine died in one incident. UNESCO also identified at least 15 cases in which journalists were allegedly shot despite visible press markings or clearly identified media vehicles.

The survey counted 62 deaths in countries outside conflict zones, the lowest such figure in 18 years. It found that 104 journalists were killed while covering armed conflicts, 38 while reporting on politics and 22 during crime-related coverage, with additional deaths linked to corruption reporting. Sylvie Couldray, UNESCO’s chief for freedom of expression and media development and media and information literacy, said the risks attached to some subjects can approach those faced in war reporting.

Brazil recorded one death: Luís Augusto Carneiro da Costa, known as Luisinho Costa, killed at Rádio Guarany’s studio in Abaetetuba, Pará, in May 2025. Pará Civil Police said the inquiry was completed and forwarded to the judiciary, with four people indicted.

UNESCO’s longer-term figures show 1,641 journalists killed since 2006, but only 223 cases ending in judicial resolution. It calculated that 86% remained unresolved or lacked an official response, one percentage point higher than in 2024.

What happened

Chinese-backed gold mining has rapidly reshaped Mayaya, a small town in Bolivia’s Amazon, creating an economy of mines, machinery, restaurants and migrant labour while bringing allegations of unlawful foreign control, mercury pollution, poor pay and large-scale illegal exports.

Chinese investors, miners and equipment importers now dominate much of Mayaya’s gold industry. The town has roughly 20 open-pit mines and 12 dredge boats, employing about 2,000 Bolivian and 200 Chinese workers. Its main street has filled with hotels, mining-supply shops and Chinese restaurants, while businesses exchange Chinese renminbi and Bolivian currency.

The expansion followed a major influx in 2017, after China restricted domestic small-scale gold mining. The Guardian’s reporting describes private Chinese companies operating through Bolivian mining cooperatives. Under Bolivia’s legal framework, foreign firms must meet registration requirements and participate in state-involved contracts; campaigners and mining researcher Alfredo Zaconeta say some investors instead use cooperatives as fronts, controlling sites, equipment, workers, gold exports and as much as 75% to 80% of profits.

The economic gains have not been shared equally. Two dredge-boat miners said Bolivian workers receive about one-quarter of the pay of Chinese workers performing comparable jobs. Bolivia’s mining regulator estimates the country produces about 100 tonnes of gold annually, but only around 10% is sold legally, costing the government and the public billions in revenue. Chinese middleman He Zijian said gold from Mayaya is moved to La Paz and smuggled onward to Dubai, where it may enter international supply chains.

Environmental and community effects are also intensifying. Mining uses mercury, and residents of surrounding Indigenous communities are increasingly affected by contamination while agriculture and fishing become harder amid deforestation, wildfires and climate pressure. Artisanal miners have moved into Madidi national park, and a village south of Mayaya recently seized and burned machinery allegedly belonging to a Chinese company. Nearby municipalities have banned gold mining.

Regulator Jaime Sanabria estimates that about 80% of Bolivia’s mining is illegal. Zaconeta says inspections are weak and investors can receive advance warning of audits. The Ministry of Mining and Metallurgy, Shenzhen Vicstar Import and Export, and Bolivia’s regional mining-cooperative federation were approached for comment, but the evidence provided no responses. Meanwhile, Bolivia’s government has moved toward fewer barriers to mining in protected and Indigenous areas, even as one early Chinese investor expects to leave Mayaya within five or six years as its gold reserves decline.

In the Philippines, embalming has gained formal training and licensing, but workers who prepare bodies for wakes still face low pay, incomplete workforce data and no seat on the licensing committee—problems affecting a service families use before burial or cremation.

Jonnel Cribello, a registered nurse who became an embalmer in 2009, now trains students at the Philippine College of Advanced Studies in Cebu, one of only four active accredited embalming schools in the country. The profession’s image began changing after the Department of Health moved to professionalize it around 2010. The profession’s size is also uncertain: an association estimated 6,000 to 6,300 licensed embalmers after exams resumed, while the health department has not recently published updated figures. What happened

China’s Yellow River is being presented as cleaner and more manageable after decades of conservation, with the clearest visible change at Hukou Waterfall in Shanxi Province and reported benefits for flood safety, water quality, farming and tourism.

At Hukou Waterfall, water that older images showed as muddy and turbulent now appears clear, according to an observation from Zhengzhou published on Nov. 2. An aerial photograph taken in Jixian County on Oct. 28, 2026, showed a rainbow above the falls. Photographer Lyu Guiming, who has recorded the site since 1999, said clear cascades had become his principal subject over the past decade.

The change is attributed to about 80 years of conservation and coordinated management of the 5,464-kilometre river, which runs through nine provinces and autonomous regions before reaching the Bohai Sea. Historical records cited in the account say the lower reaches suffered more than 1,500 dyke breaches and overflows and changed course 26 times before 1949.

Flood-control measures now include 1,371 kilometres of dykes along the lower reaches and 40 reservoirs with a combined capacity of 19 billion cubic metres. Wei Xiangyang, chief engineer of the Yellow River Conservancy Commission, said the projects had prevented an estimated 10.09 trillion yuan in flood-related economic losses over the past eight decades.

Conservation work has also restored 290,500 square kilometres affected by soil erosion and kept approximately 24.7 billion tonnes of sediment out of the river. Since a unified management system covering the river’s provincial-level regions began in 1999, the river has maintained continuous flow for 27 years, while improved allocation has supported 8.8 million hectares of irrigated farmland and annual grain production of about 89.5 billion kilograms.

Yellow River Conservancy Commission data show that monitoring sections rated Class I to III increased from 59.1 percent in 2016 to 93.2 percent in 2025. In Jixian County, Lyu’s widely circulated waterfall photographs have also contributed to tourism becoming a pillar industry in a county previously described as impoverished.

Does Scaling Toward AGI Depend on What System-1 Lets System-2 Execute?
science_cs_aipolitics_and_powertechnology_and_aiscience anchorpreprint_not_peer_reviewed
What happened

A proposed approach to scaling toward artificial general intelligence would pair a lightweight predictor with a costly reasoning system, allowing the predictor to screen prompts, experiments, tool calls and other interactions before they consume substantial resources. The claimed prize is more useful capability per unit of compute, data, latency and real-world effort.

The idea comes from a preprint by Qi Wang and colleagues, posted on arXiv as version 1 on 29 October 2026. Its answer to the title’s question is a qualified yes: future progress may depend not only on making a powerful “System-2” larger, but also on designing a “System-1” that decides when System-2 should act.

System-2 is used here for the resource-intensive component responsible for reasoning, planning, decisions, function calls or computer use. System-1 is a cheaper, semantically aware predictor. It estimates the likely utility of a candidate interaction—such as a prompt, training rollout, physical task, query or tool invocation—and System-2 executes only candidates that pass an adaptive threshold. The resulting outcomes then provide feedback for improving System-1.

The authors call this a conditional-interaction loop: predict, gate, execute and update. The systems are intended to be co-designed rather than assembled as a fixed model cascade. System-1 may allocate training examples, rollout budgets, replay storage, context or inference effort, while System-2’s results continually change what System-1 should consider valuable.

Why interaction selection becomes a scaling problem

The preprint argues that simply increasing the number of interactions can become inefficient because their value is uneven. In reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, for example, a model may generate multiple reasoning attempts for a prompt even when all receive the same score. Such a prompt consumes rollouts but supplies no relative policy-gradient signal under the described training setup.

The same concern is extended to reasoning tokens that do not change a relevant belief, physical-agent experiences that repeat known information, and self-generated tasks an agent has already mastered. Physical experiments, robotic actions and specialized measurements add another constraint: they may take time that additional computing power cannot remove.

This motivates the proposed quantity rho_u, the share of candidate interactions whose utility exceeds a declared threshold. Utility is application-dependent. It might mean information gain, expected learning progress, decision value, task improvement or adaptation risk; the authors stress that these are not interchangeable. A high-information interaction is not automatically the one that improves task performance or robustness.

Under ideal screening and equal execution costs, the paper says the amount of execution compute could fall by a factor approaching 1/rho_u. That is a theoretical limit, not a reported system-wide result. Predictor mistakes, the cost of screening, exploration and changes in the data distribution all reduce the saving. The paper also derives a regret bound linking poor selection to prediction error and the margin between useful and less useful candidates.

From total compute to informative compute

The central hypothesis is that rho_u could become a measurable efficiency coordinate for scaling. Traditional scaling discussions emphasize parameters, data and total compute. This framework instead asks how much of those resources is spent on interactions that matter for the selected objective.

The distinction matters because a gate does not enlarge the available data pool. Rather, it can increase the share of paid interactions that are useful. A perfect gate would avoid executing candidates classified as uninformative, while still paying the smaller cost of screening them. The authors therefore propose measuring both utility obtained per screened candidate and utility obtained per unit of total system cost, including screening and execution.

The framework is intended to cover training, test-time reasoning, adaptive decision-making, model routing and agentic tool use. A static fast model that routes difficult requests to a larger model is treated as a partial analogue. The proposed version differs because System-1 is updated from System-2’s outcomes and can schedule resources throughout training and deployment, not just choose a model at inference time.

The paper also presents a “super parrot” trajectory in which AI increasingly generates its own instructions, reasoning traces, feedback and experiments. This is explicitly described as a hypothesis and illustrative roadmap rather than an established chronology. Its implication is that self-generated activity could make internal value judgment more important: an agent must decide which of its own proposed interactions are worth funding with its limited compute.

What the preprint claims—and what remains conditional

The authors distinguish four levels of argument. They present interaction efficiency as a possible future bottleneck; advocate co-designing System-1 and System-2; hypothesize that utility-relevant interaction density affects scaling efficiency; and predict that this density may decline as systems improve, contributing to diminishing returns.

The proposal is therefore a research framework, not evidence that the approach has achieved AGI or that the predicted scaling behavior has been demonstrated across systems. Its benefits depend on having a sufficiently reliable utility predictor. The preprint acknowledges that a gate cannot replace exploration when the predictor is uncertain, and that information gain, learning progress, decision value and robustness may lead to different choices.

As a not-peer-reviewed preprint, the work offers formal definitions, bounds and a design hypothesis for future evaluation. Its main shift is conceptual: progress might come not only from scaling the executor, but from learning which interactions deserve execution in the first place.

SimpleLoRIF simplifies the earlier LoRIF attribution approach by removing its curvature adjustment and calculating the relevant signal through the final four transformer blocks. Wizard is a preprint from researchers at Tencent, the National University of Singapore and Nanyang Technological University that presents a video-based game world model for real-time control of unfamiliar scenes, characters and motions, with potential to let players reshape environments and direct mechanics-consistent combat.

The system is designed to address limitations the authors identify in existing game world models: many support only simple settings, predefined actions or motion patterns, generalize poorly beyond their training environments and struggle to provide responsive interaction. Wizard separates game-state management and gameplay logic from visual generation, then uses those structured states to guide a video model.

Players can provide scene images, character references or natural-language instructions. The system reconstructs an approximate traversable environment from a scene image, represents its geometry through depth and height information, and converts characters into 3D meshes with controllable articulated skeletons. These representations become visual proxies that guide the generation of each new video segment.

The paper, posted on arXiv as version 1 on 29 October 2026, reports that Wizard can operate at 24 frames per second after an initialization stage that takes less than five minutes. The authors present this as a research result from a preprint, not as an independently validated product or established capability.

Structured control replaces fixed game logic

Wizard maintains an explicit world state containing character positions, poses, health, action phases and skill cooldowns. A behavior planner uses that state to select movements and attacks for a boss character. It can move the boss toward a player, choose attacks based on the player’s position and geometry, and enforce timing constraints through a rule-based controller.

Combat outcomes are handled separately from video appearance. The controller checks attack hitboxes against target geometry, accounts for invulnerability periods, reduces health after valid hits and triggers reactions. When a character’s health reaches zero, the system ends the combat sequence and displays the outcome. This arrangement is intended to preserve game rules even though the visible world is rendered by a generative video model.

The same state-management approach supports scene manipulation. Natural-language instructions are translated into programs that apply predefined geometric operations, such as raising a platform or inverting an environment. The modified geometry is rendered from the current camera position and passed to the video generator, allowing the paper’s demonstrations to combine changes to characters and surroundings during a single scenario.

For character control, the framework detects body structure from a reference image, segments the character, reconstructs a mesh and generates or selects a compatible skeleton. A motion library supplies animations that can be retargeted to characters with different body proportions. The authors say the system can retrieve movements according to both anatomical compatibility and intended style, then filter out motions with problems such as poor ground contact or unnatural limb intersections.

Training data and video generation

The researchers assembled 50.3 hours of aligned proxy-and-video data covering 750 scenes and 178 characters. The material combines recorded gameplay, synthetic scenes, Unreal Engine simulations and the WildWorld public dataset. It includes scene geometry, camera information and character skeletons so that the visual proxies remain aligned with the video.

The dataset includes 8.8 hours of Elden Ring footage, 11.6 hours of procedurally generated data, 3.3 hours from Unreal Engine simulations and 26.6 hours from WildWorld. The authors describe the sources as covering different environments, body structures, camera movements and combat interactions. The paper notes that Elden Ring footage was recorded by the authors for research, while WildWorld-derived data was used under a non-commercial research license and not redistributed.

Wizard builds on the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video model. Its proxy representation encodes scene height and depth as well as character skeletons, while reference images provide character appearance. An adaptive attention mask links each reference image to the corresponding character’s current location, limiting appearance guidance to that region and reducing the chance that one character’s identity will bleed into another’s.

To make generation responsive, the authors adapt the model for autoregressive operation and apply consistency and distribution-matching distillation. The resulting system uses two denoising steps and retains a limited recent-frame context through a key-value cache. The paper reports real-time rollouts at 24 FPS.

Reported demonstrations and remaining limits

The evaluation described in the preprint is qualitative. The authors use user-provided scene and character references that were unseen during training, then show generated combat sequences and creative interactions without additional fine-tuning. Their examples cover new combinations of scenes, characters and motions, character transformation during play, scene inversion and the addition of new environmental elements.

The paper presents these demonstrations as evidence of zero-shot generalization and mechanics-consistent gameplay, but it also identifies important limits. Wizard depends on a motion library, making it harder to find suitable movements for characters with uncommon body structures. The consistency between scene proxies and generated video can decline during longer rollouts. The authors say future work will target more flexible character motion and stronger long-term scene consistency.
