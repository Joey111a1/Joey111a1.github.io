Brazil: Senate approves Public Security PEC without reducing the age of criminal responsibility

What happened

Brazil’s Senate approved a constitutional amendment on Tuesday the 3rd to reorganize public security and intensify action against organized crime, while leaving the country’s criminal-responsibility age at 18 and sending proposed age changes to separate proceedings.

The Public Security PEC passed both rounds with unanimous support among voting senators: 73–0 in the first and 75–0 in the second, exceeding the 49 votes required for a constitutional amendment. The text, authored by the executive branch, had already been approved by the Chamber of Deputies and is expected to be promulgated by Congress.

The amendment would constitutionalize the Unified Public Security System and require federal, state and municipal institutions to work through coordinated, decentralized cooperation. It proposes tougher penalties and stricter prison regimes for leaders of criminal factions and militias, including placement in maximum-security facilities and limits on sentence progression. It also provides for measures targeting criminal assets, liability for companies involved in crime, and protection for whistleblowers and their families.

Other provisions would expand the Federal Highway Police’s responsibilities, permit the creation of civilian municipal police forces under external oversight by public prosecutors, and establish a national penal-policy system. The proposal would also require half of the National Public Security Fund and National Penitentiary Fund resources to be transferred to states and municipalities, excluding specified national penitentiary-police expenses. It additionally proposes directing 10% of annual financial surpluses from the Social Fund linked to pre-salt oil revenues to security funds.

The age-of-responsibility dispute remains separate

Before the vote, senators considered amendments to lower criminal responsibility from 18 to 16, or in some cases to 14 for serious offenses. The rapporteur, Rogério Carvalho, did not include them in the PEC. Instead, senators approved by 38–36 a motion to separate them into another proposal that could proceed independently.

One amendment sought a general age of 16, with 14 for crimes including torture, drug trafficking, terrorism and participation in criminal organizations. Another would have retained 18 as the general threshold but lowered it to 16 for heinous crimes. The Senate’s decision means those changes were not part of the approved Public Security PEC. President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has promised to create a Ministry of Public Security after its approval.

Chinese central bank shifts policy rate anchor to overnight
china_domesticglobal_south_and_exploitedpolitics_and_powereconomy_and_businessregional expansion
What happened

China’s central bank moved its main policy-rate reference from the seven-day reverse repo rate to the overnight rate on Monday, Nov. 2. The People’s Bank of China (PBOC) supplied 541 billion yuan (about $80.6 billion) through overnight reverse repos, priced at 1.25 percent using fixed-rate, quantity-based bidding. In these transactions, the bank acquires securities from financial institutions and agrees to return them the following day, providing liquidity. The operation was the first conducted under a mechanism announced late last month.

Under the revised framework, overnight reverse repos will be carried out every working day and become the PBOC’s principal open-market tool. Temporary repos and other reverse-repo operations whose pricing had followed the seven-day rate will be tied instead to the overnight rate, without changing their existing spreads. Analysts said the change supports a shift toward interest-rate-based regulation. They said the shorter benchmark could clarify policy signals, limit interruptions around tax-payment periods and month-ends, and improve the passage of rate changes through money markets to bond yields and lending costs.

China: Hepatitis B does not mean panic; expert interprets key points of treatment and prevention for liver cirrhosis
china_domesticmaterial_harmregional expansion
What happened

Hepatologist Lu Yinying has urged people living with chronic hepatitis B not to regard the diagnosis as a prediction of cirrhosis or liver cancer, while stressing that silent liver damage, limited standardized care and delayed testing can allow disease progression.

Lu, a senior hepatology specialist at the General Hospital of the PLA and a deputy director of the Beijing Medical Association’s hepatology branch, recently discussed prevention and treatment on the People’s Good Doctor science video program. She said hepatitis B is not an inevitable route to cirrhosis or cancer: timely diagnosis, monitoring and treatment can help most patients avoid those outcomes.

She emphasized that treatment controls the virus and liver damage rather than curing hepatitis B. Antiviral therapy can suppress viral replication, while doctors may consider additional anti-fibrotic treatment when fibrosis or other risk factors persist. Mild to moderate fibrosis may be reversible if its cause is controlled; established cirrhosis is much harder to reverse, shifting the focus to slowing progression and preventing complications.

Because early liver damage often causes no symptoms, Lu recommended combining liver-stiffness assessment with liver-function tests and ultrasound, with biopsy when necessary. She also warned that normal liver-function results do not exclude fibrosis. Regular reviews, avoiding alcohol, taking medicines consistently and avoiding unapproved remedies are central to management; stopping antivirals without medical advice can trigger viral rebound and severe liver injury.

United States: Take CBD or THC gummies for sleep or wellness? What a new federal ban means for you

What happened

A federal restriction scheduled to take effect in the United States on December 11 would remove many hemp-derived CBD and THC gummies, drinks, oils and other products from the market, affecting consumers who use them for sleep, pain, epilepsy or alternatives to alcohol.

The measure was included in a broad budget law passed last year. A bipartisan group of senators delayed its original November implementation date by one month, but another extension remains uncertain. It sets a limit of 0.4 milligrams of THC per container for hemp-derived products. Because many gummies and beverages contain several milligrams per serving, the threshold would effectively eliminate most full-spectrum products, which contain the plant’s naturally occurring cannabinoids, including small amounts of THC.

The restriction does not cover cannabis products sold through state-licensed medical or recreational marijuana dispensaries. Consumers elsewhere would need to examine package labels and consider where products are sold. Hemp-derived products containing synthetic cannabinoids, including commercially produced delta-8 and delta-10 THC, face a separate prohibition scheduled for November 12.

For people who rely on these products, the consequences could be immediate. Adrian Bruneau, a former US Marine Corps aviator who served in the Balkans and Middle East, said CBD gummies helped him sleep after post-traumatic stress disorder contributed to alcohol and cocaine use following his active service. He later said THC beverages replaced alcohol. Bruneau is a cofounder of the American Alternative Care Policy Network, which opposes the restriction.

Advocates say veterans, first responders, trauma survivors and people with chronic pain or epilepsy could lose products they have incorporated into daily routines. Businesses warn that hundreds of thousands of jobs and tens of billions of dollars could be exposed, though that is an industry forecast rather than an observed loss. Supporters of the restriction point to the rapid spread of high-dose and synthetic products and concerns about children’s access.

CBD and THC do not have officially approved medical uses in most of the conditions described, and the evidence is mixed. Cannabis can impair cognition, worsen sleep for some users, cause severe vomiting and increase risks during pregnancy; higher-potency and frequent use are associated with addiction and mental-health harms. Bipartisan proposals in Congress would give states more flexibility, but their outcome is unresolved.

Advances in Agentic Computer Graphics
science_cs_aiglobal_south_and_exploitedpolitics_and_powertechnology_and_aiscience anchorpreprint_not_peer_reviewed
What happened

A preprint by researchers from the University of Texas at Austin, Stanford University, Simon Fraser University, the University of California, Berkeley, and the University of Cambridge surveys systems that use language-model agents to build and revise graphics. It proposes a shared framework for comparing their tools, feedback loops, representations, and remaining reliability problems.

The study examines a shift from one-shot graphics generation toward workflows in which an agent selects operations, runs them through graphics software, observes the outcome, and adjusts its next step. The systems covered can work with 2D media, static or animated 3D content, materials, lighting, cameras, simulations, and other graphics tasks.

The authors describe agentic graphics as systems meeting three conditions: a language model must influence at least some actions; those actions must be carried out in a graphics environment; and intermediate results or execution feedback must affect later decisions. This definition excludes single-pass language-model output and methods that use feedback only for training or final assessment.

The report is a survey and organizational contribution rather than a presentation of one new graphics model or benchmark result. Its collected literature exceeds 300 papers and extends through September 2026, according to the authors. They say the field’s growth makes manual review difficult, while different areas—including CAD, animation, and simulation—often use different terms for comparable planning and feedback functions.

A framework for comparing agentic systems

The proposed taxonomy organizes systems along four dimensions: specification, base model, harness, and graphics interface and tooling. Specification covers the task, supplied material, and desired result. A system might receive language, an image, video, an existing 3D scene, or a document, then return executable code, a structured description, an editable scene, or rendered media.

The base model is the component that interprets requirements and proposes actions. The survey distinguishes text-oriented language models, vision-language models, and systems combining different models. It also records whether models are used without task-specific adaptation, adjusted through supervised training, or optimized through reinforcement-learning methods.

The harness forms the operational link between the model and graphics software. It schedules work, sends instructions to tools, brings back observations, and retains information that may be useful later. The authors classify workflows by how tasks are organized—such as sequential, parallel, or dependency-based processes—and by how revisions occur. A system may regenerate an output, alter only part of it, restore an earlier state, revise its plan, or choose among competing candidates.

Feedback is a defining feature. It can come from the acting system itself, from a separate critic, from rule-based or programmatic checks, from the graphics environment, or from a person. Signals may include visual comparisons, scores, rankings, diagnoses, structural scene information, compilation errors, or simulation results. This feedback is intended to guide subsequent actions rather than merely judge the final product.

Tools, interfaces, and representations

The survey separates what an agent does from how it communicates that action. Some systems produce scripts; others provide structured specifications, invoke predefined functions, or control software through a graphical interface. Script-based approaches allow detailed control over sequencing and parameter relationships, but generated programs may contain syntax or application-interface errors. Structured interfaces constrain the available operations, while GUI control exposes existing workflows but depends on software state such as the current selection or editing mode.

The authors also distinguish three kinds of graphics environment. An agent-native environment is built around machine-directed operation and explicit state. An agent-adapted environment adds an agent-facing layer to existing software, including facilities for inspecting scenes or managing edits. A general-purpose environment relies on ordinary scripting APIs or GUI workflows without a dedicated agent abstraction.

Representations determine what can be edited and what feedback can reveal. The survey covers program code, explicit geometry, changing simulation variables, scene layouts and relationships, pixels, and learned or latent representations. A single workflow may use several of these at once—for example, a construction program, an editable scene, and rendered images. Images can show visual differences, but they do not directly expose every hidden geometric, topological, or physical property.

A growing field with open design problems

Using the taxonomy, the associated literature system searches for relevant papers, examines their text and figures, and records classifications, design observations, limitations, and supporting evidence. The authors present this system as assistance for researchers who need to organize a rapidly changing field and incorporate new studies, not as a substitute for human analysis.

The report identifies continuing challenges in coordinating agents with one another and with graphics tools, creating interfaces for effective human–agent collaboration, sustaining planning and correction over long tasks, and evaluating interactive systems reliably. It also highlights a basic difficulty in automation: successful execution does not necessarily mean that the resulting graphics satisfy the intended visual or geometric requirements, and some observations may fail to reveal hidden errors.

The preprint therefore presents agentic graphics as an emerging research design space rather than a settled replacement for conventional authoring. Its taxonomy is intended to make systems with different terminology and technical interfaces easier to compare, while the survey’s identified challenges leave the reliability, evaluation, and practical scope of these workflows open for further research.

Wave Forcing: Towards Speed-of-Light Streaming Video Generation
science_cs_aiglobal_south_and_exploitedpolitics_and_powertechnology_and_aiscience anchorpreprint_not_peer_reviewed
What happened

Wave Forcing is a preprint from researchers affiliated with DENG Lab at Shanghai Jiao Tong University and other Chinese universities that proposes a pipeline for continuous long-video generation, combining a new denoising schedule, specialized training and multi-GPU execution to increase motion and throughput without sacrificing subject stability.

The work targets a problem in real-time video generation: systems must produce successive chunks continuously while keeping appearance, motion and scene history coherent. The authors say existing chunk-wise autoregressive approaches often require separate forward passes for denoising and refreshing the clean history used by later chunks. Longer generations can also lose quality or develop camera drift.

The proposed method, Wave Forcing (WF), lets newer video chunks use cleaner intermediate states from earlier chunks before those earlier chunks have completed all their denoising stages. It arranges the work as a wavefront: different full-model replicas handle staggered denoising and history-cache updates concurrently. After the pipeline is filled, the design aims to complete one output chunk during each concurrent forward round while retaining every denoising update.

WF also changes how the model is trained. Its three-stage recipe introduces mixed-noise denoising, teacher-guided motion and framing supervision, and causal adaptation using histories generated by the model itself. The authors argue that this is necessary because a model trained only for conventional sequential generation may not behave reliably when it receives partially denoised, self-generated context during streaming inference.

A joint design for generation and execution

The accompanying Wave Runtime, or WaveRT, is intended to turn the scheduling idea into practical throughput. Its Wave Parallelism system delivers key/value attention data early, overlaps communication with computation, and writes data directly into attention buffers. Optimized kernels and pipelined variational autoencoder decoding are used to reduce delays between denoising and producing playable video.

The experiments use Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B and compare four- and five-step WF with Rolling Forcing and Causal Forcing. For video quality, the authors generate short clips at 832×480 resolution and 16 frames per second, and 100-second clips from 200 MovieGen prompts. They supplement VBench scores with measures of subject preservation and camera drift because high motion scores can coexist with a subject leaving the frame or unstable camera movement.

In the reported five-step comparison, WF scores 81.64 on VBench Total and 84.40 on VBench Quality, compared with 81.15 and 83.77 for Rolling Forcing. Its long-video quality score is 81.26, close to Rolling Forcing’s 81.18. WF’s Dynamic Degree is substantially higher: 91.45 on 100-second videos, versus 36.63 for Rolling Forcing. Subject Preservation is 97.10, while Camera Drift is 0.37; Rolling Forcing records 92.75 and 0.17 respectively.

The comparison also illustrates why the authors do not treat motion as a sufficient measure of quality. Causal Forcing reaches a higher long-video Dynamic Degree of 96.61 and quality score of 82.15, but its Subject Preservation score is 71.11 and Camera Drift is 11.14. In the authors’ evaluation, the subject disappears at points in the sample, leaving background objects visible. WF therefore presents its result as a balance among motion, quality and stability rather than as the highest score on every individual metric.

Reported speed and remaining limits

On eight H200 GPUs, four-step WF reaches 147.6 frames per second for the diffusion transformer portion and 134.8 frames per second end to end. The paper reports this as 7.07 times the end-to-end throughput of its single-GPU baseline. In tests across model sizes from 1.3 billion to 14 billion parameters, Wave Parallelism is faster than the quicker of the Ulysses and Ring context-parallel baselines at most tested settings, with the largest reported advantage under a SageAttention plus 8-bit-weight-and-activation configuration reaching 2.53 times.

The system-efficiency results require important qualification. The corresponding DiT runtime tests use dummy, randomly initialized weights because the authors say suitable pretrained 14-billion-parameter weights and sufficient training resources were unavailable. The measurements also exclude loading and file encoding. The paper notes that VAE decoding remains a bottleneck: for the 1.3B model, the four-step configuration reaches 147.58 DiT FPS but 134.81 end-to-end FPS.

The authors identify further constraints. Wave Parallelism uses one full-model replica for each denoising stage plus a cache-refresh stage, so its pipeline width is tied to the sampling-step count. Its throughput advantage narrows for larger models. The evaluation uses fixed conditioning and does not establish low input-to-visible-output latency, responsiveness to prompt changes, multi-stream service guarantees or performance in interactive camera and action-controlled settings.

This is an arXiv preprint, not peer-reviewed research. Its reported findings are therefore presented as the authors’ experimental results and proposed system design, with broader validation still needed across models, hardware, workloads and interactive use cases.

What happened

SWE-Trail is a preprint method for collecting coding-agent training data more efficiently: it uses earlier attempts on a repository issue to choose where to investigate further, while requiring the agent to generate actions without seeing that history.

Coding agents solve repository issues through extended cycles of exploration, code changes and validation. Their interaction records are increasingly used for post-training, but independently sampling several attempts for the same issue can produce diminishing returns: later attempts often revisit the same files and decisions. The researchers argue that previous attempts can instead reveal where additional collection may be useful, without giving the agent advice that will be unavailable during testing.

The method, developed by researchers affiliated with the University of Connecticut and Ant Group, works in two stages.
