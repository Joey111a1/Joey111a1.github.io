From a quiet life to the terror of armed gangs: The story of Haiti’s Kenscoff district
global_front_pagematerial_harmpolitics_and_powerregional expansion
What happened

Haiti’s Kenscoff district has been transformed by armed-gang attacks that killed 164 people, displaced residents and disrupted a farming area supplying Port-au-Prince. The violence also left hostages, destroyed homes and businesses, and exposed severe gaps in security preparations, according to a human-rights investigation.

Kenscoff lies about 20 kilometres southeast of downtown Port-au-Prince, in the heights of Pétion-Ville, and is home largely to peasant farmers. Its terraced fields supply fruit, vegetables and flowers to markets, grocery chains and restaurants in the capital. The area was also known for day trips, horse riding and visits to Fort Jacques. Ranch le Montcel was burned during gang violence in 2025 and remains closed, while Chalet Le Refuge has also shut.

That life was disrupted by attacks on July 7–8 and August 23–24. Houses were burned, people were killed, and others were kidnapped or forced to flee. The community had already suffered an attack from January 27–29, 2025, when residents sought shelter at the town hall. They were later moved to a school in the Robin area, which was considered safer but was attacked in July.

Rosy Auguste Ducéna, programme director of the National Human Rights Defense Network (RNDDH), said the organization gathered testimony from local officials, police, self-defence members, about 100 survivors and relatives. She said armed groups built barricades to block police armoured vehicles before attacking Robin, where people were shot, burned and killed and the school shelter was set on fire.

The RNDDH investigation also blamed government and senior security officials for failing to respond to warnings. The Kenscoff police chief reportedly requested more armoured vehicles and operational support on July 27 and 28, but received neither. The report says the police presence was then cut by nearly 60 percent, while gangs used local assistance and surveillance drones before the August assault.

During that attack, which began around 10 p.m., gangs entered neighbourhoods and the town centre, attacking residents and a church shelter. Ducéna said women and girls were raped and people were killed or burned. More than 60 people, including children, babies, elderly people and sick residents, were kidnapped. UNICEF-supported mediation helped secure the release of several children and babies, but dozens reportedly remained captive.

Police operations later released six hostages, according to the Haitian National Police, but authorities gave no account of the others. Although daily life is slowly resuming, fear persists, while gangs continue to threaten nearby areas.

Planetary extremes are ‘off the charts’ as world’s biggest climate talks kick off
global_front_pagematerial_harmpolitics_and_powermajor supplement
What happened

The World Meteorological Organization warned that climate extremes endured through 2026 are likely to persist into 2027, while a strengthening El Niño could intensify heat, floods, drought and fires as COP31 opens in Antalya, Türkiye, with deaths, food production and livelihoods already affected.

The WMO analysis examined data from January 2025 through September 2026. It found that the previous 12 years were the warmest 12 on record, while January-to-September temperatures in 2026 were 1.47 degrees Celsius above the preindustrial period.

WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo said heatwaves, tropical cyclones and rainstorms were intensifying faster than historical baselines anticipated. The report says the current El Niño could become the strongest on record, continue into 2027 and increase the likelihood of extreme weather; some scientists predict 2027 could be the hottest year recorded.

Carbon dioxide, methane and nitrous oxide reached record levels in 2025 and continued rising this year, while sea levels and ocean temperatures also reached new highs. Glaciers lost enough ice in 2025 to fill five Olympic-sized swimming pools every second for a year.

The human consequences are already visible: heatwaves in Europe this summer were estimated to have killed more than 50,000 people, and a rock-and-ice avalanche on the Nepal-China border killed more than 1,300. Flooding in Vietnam and low monsoon rainfall in India have damaged food production, eroded livelihoods and increased poverty.

A fine would not immediately affect service, but withdrawing spectrum could mean slower data, congestion or poorer quality during busy periods. In the most serious scenario, customers might need to switch providers, while recovery of unused prepaid balances could depend on Simba’s financial position.

PDE #1
Near-sightedness Prevention is a Tug-of-War in Growth
china_domesticmaterial_harmtechnology_and_ai_societyregional expansion
What happened

Myopia prevalence has remained high in China in recent years, while rising prescription levels among children are causing concern among parents. Myopia-control lenses are increasingly being chosen for young people who need glasses, but misconceptions remain. Song Hongxin, chief physician at Beijing Tongren Hospital, describes two common designs: defocus micro-structure lenses, which create a peripheral image in front of the retina, and lenses that reduce peripheral contrast through a less-clear outer zone. Both are intended to slow axial eye elongation and the progression of myopia.

Song said clinicians generally no longer recommend ordinary single-vision lenses alone for most myopic children, reporting a progression rate of about 100 diopters a year with such lenses, with some cases faster. He said myopia-control lenses can delay worsening, but do not stop it completely and their effect varies by child. He urged parents to assess products through rigorous clinical evidence, especially multicenter randomized trials and prospective studies, rather than brand claims. The interview also linked increasingly early, severe and rapid-onset myopia among minors to longer near-work and screen exposure, academic pressure and insufficient outdoor time; it described prevention as a long-term balance between healthier eye use and those pressures, alongside parental genetics.

A supersolid has two properties that ordinarily seem incompatible: an orderly, repeating structure like a crystal and the flow characteristics of a superfluid, whose internal resistance can vanish under the right conditions. Supersolidity was experimentally observed in ultracold atomic Bose–Einstein condensates in 2017, but those systems generally require temperatures close to absolute zero.

The new experiment used a different platform. Researchers from the University of Warsaw’s Faculty of Physics, the Military University of Technology in Poland, IBM Research Zurich and France’s National Centre for Scientific Research created a device containing a liquid-crystal microcavity and an organic semiconductor. Instead of ultracold atoms, the system produced exciton polaritons—short-lived particles formed when light and matter become strongly coupled.

The microcavity could operate at temperatures approaching 60°C, according to Professor Wiktor Piecek of the Military University of Technology. The researchers reported that the resulting particles formed a condensate with supersolid behavior without the extreme cooling normally associated with such experiments.

Evidence for the state came from light emitted by the sample. When the system entered the supersolid phase, regularly spaced bright fringes appeared, indicating a repeating variation in particle density. The position of those fringes changed between laser pulses, suggesting that the system was selecting among several equivalent arrangements—a phenomenon known as spontaneous symmetry breaking.

The team also observed a repeating pattern in the polarization of the emitted light, called a persistent spin helix. The intensity pattern and polarization pattern represented two forms of order occurring simultaneously, helping identify the unusual quantum state.

The experiment faced a practical limitation: the organic semiconductor gradually reacted with the liquid crystal, leaving each sample usable for only a few hours. Measurements had to be planned in advance, since repeating one required fabricating a new sample. The crucial device layers were produced and measured through work involving IBM Research Zurich and the Military University of Technology.

The findings, published in *Science Advances*, establish a room-temperature and electrically tunable platform for studying supersolid exciton-polariton condensates. Applying voltage can change the system’s optical properties, giving researchers more control than earlier approaches described in the account.

Possible applications include ultrafast photonic devices, neuromorphic computing systems and optical sensors sensitive to small changes in polarization. These remain prospective uses, but avoiding cryogenic cooling could make such technologies more practical to investigate and develop.

Laboratory experiments indicated that more viscous particles resisted bacterial breakdown and fragmentation for longer. Computer simulations then suggested that they could sink farther before disintegrating. Using those findings, the team calculated that highly viscous marine snow may carry over six times more carbon into the deep ocean than less viscous particles.

A companion study in *Nature Communications* described the imaging approach. The researchers said it provides a way to examine the microscopic physical environments where bacteria interact with mucus—an interaction also relevant to coral reefs, kelp forests, plant roots and animal digestive systems.

The findings add particle structure and viscosity to scientists’ understanding of the ocean carbon cycle. They do not imply that all marine snow stores carbon equally: composition, structure and the conditions encountered during sinking vary from particle to particle.

Artemis IV is intended to carry astronauts to the Moon in 2028. SpaceX’s approach would use Starship, refueled in Earth orbit before traveling toward the Moon. Keeping both systems in development gives NASA an alternative as testing continues, according to the agency.

NASA also reported that preparation timelines for Artemis III hardware have been reduced by about 50%. NASA and Axiom Space have developed a lighter suit called Sortie for early lunar excursions; it weighs about half as much as Axiom’s primary suit. NASA presents these measures as steps toward longer missions near the lunar south pole, a lasting human presence on the Moon and, eventually, crewed missions to Mars.

Scientists discover a surprising link between acorns and lyme disease
science_generalpublic_healthclimate_disasterscience anchorinstitutional_press_release
What happened

A 35-year ecological study in Dutchess County, New York, suggests that Lyme disease risk is shaped less by deer than by interactions among mice, oak trees, ticks, other wildlife and weather. The findings could improve forecasts of tick surges, although climate effects remain difficult to predict.

Researchers at the Cary Institute of Ecosystem Studies have tracked ticks, mammals, oak trees, predators and weather on a 2,000-acre forested property. The study, published in the *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, combines nine findings from the long-running project and illustrates how decades of observation can reveal patterns that brief studies may overlook.

One central result concerns deer, long assumed to drive tick increases because adult female ticks feed on them. Researchers found no statistically significant link at the site between the number of deer and the abundance of nymphal blacklegged ticks, the immature ticks responsible for much of Lyme disease transmission to people. The researchers did observe a weak relationship in shorter portions of the data, but it disappeared when the full record was examined.

Mice were a stronger predictor. The following year brought approximately 40% more nymphal ticks after years when white-footed mouse populations were especially large. Mice provide effective blood meals for tick larvae and efficiently transmit *Borrelia burgdorferi*, the bacterium that causes Lyme disease.

The connection extends back to oak trees. In years when oak trees produce exceptionally large, synchronized acorn crops, known as mast years, the trees provide an unusually abundant food supply. The additional food can increase rodent populations the following year, which in turn supports more young ticks. Researchers found that heavy acorn production could predict a rise in nymphal ticks two years later, potentially offering an earlier warning of elevated exposure.

However, more mice did not consistently mean a higher percentage of infected ticks. Skunks, squirrels and opossums also host feeding ticks but are generally less effective at transmitting the bacterium. When more larvae feed on those animals, the number of infected nymphs can fall even if the total tick population rises. This means risk depends on both tick abundance and the proportion carrying the pathogen.

The long-term observations also challenged assumptions about temperature. Ticks survived intense heat and cold in natural settings, possibly by sheltering under leaves or deeper in soil. Yet warmer years were generally followed by fewer nymphal ticks, while warmer conditions could also support more mice. The opposing effects make the consequences of climate change uncertain.

Lyme disease is the most frequently reported tick-borne illness in the United States, with nearly 500,000 diagnoses reported annually in the source account. The researchers plan to continue monitoring how climate shifts, landscape changes and invasive species affect the forest community and future human exposure.
