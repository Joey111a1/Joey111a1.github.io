Myanmar committed genocide against Rohingya minority, world’s top court rules
global_front_pagepolitics_and_powermaterial_harmanchormajor supplementregional expansion
What happened

The International Court of Justice has ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya during the 2017 military campaign, finding an intent to destroy a substantial part of the minority. The judgment directs Myanmar to address the crime and its consequences, but does not ensure refugees can return safely.

Issued in The Hague on Thursday in a case brought by The Gambia, the decision was reached by a 9–6 vote. It is the first time an international court has held a state responsible for genocide. ICJ president Yuji Iwasawa said the judges found that the only reasonable explanation for Myanmar’s actions was an intent to destroy a substantial part of the Rohingya population in northern Rakhine State.

The case concerned the 2016–17 military campaign, which drove more than 700,000 mostly Muslim Rohingya across the border into Bangladesh. Reports accompanying their arrival described killings of adults and children, rape and burned villages. Myanmar denied the allegations, saying its forces had responded to attacks by armed groups.

In Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, Rohingya widow Rahama Khatun watched the judgment on her phone from her hut. Afterward, she said she was glad to hear that justice was being served. The court ordered Myanmar to investigate and penalize those responsible, provide reparations and permit refugees to return. But the ruling does not guarantee safe return for the roughly 1.2 million Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh.

The decision may also have implications for South Africa’s separate case accusing Israel of genocide against Palestinians in Gaza. Middle East Eye described it as a potentially significant precedent, particularly for how judges assess intent when a state argues that its military actions are driven by security concerns.

Clinics face rising demand as supplies dwindle
