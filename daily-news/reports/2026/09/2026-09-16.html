What happened

Festival organizers had offered her lodging there, and the Morelos prosecutor’s office said she was staying overnight at the venue. The venue said Tacoronte was attacked with a knife during an alleged robbery and that staff immediately notified authorities. Neighbors described hearing screams for help and finding her injured. According to accounts cited by El País, she was able to run and seek refuge after the stabbing, while some residents tried to chase the attacker. She later died from her injuries.

Why “El Trompas” is the main investigative target

The Morelos prosecutor’s office has treated robbery as its main hypothesis while continuing to consider other indicators and motives. Authorities are also investigating the death as a possible feminicide, a classification involving the gender-related killing of a woman. The prosecutor’s office said the case was being handled with federal agencies and that other explanations had not been ruled out.

Sources close to the investigation told El País that the main suspect is a man known as “El Trompas.” Security footage from businesses in Cuautla allegedly shows a man matching that description committing or attempting other robberies. One recording, made at about 1:15 p.m. on the same Saturday, reportedly shows a man fleeing after encountering a police patrol.

The clothing in those recordings reportedly matches witness accounts and images from nearby residents’ private security cameras: a white T-shirt, light-colored shorts and a black backpack. A separate image circulated on Facebook in connection with an alleged theft at a bicycle shop. A source linked to the investigation told El País that the person shown in the different images is the same man now being sought in connection with Tacoronte’s killing.

The identification remains part of the investigation. The supplied accounts do not report an arrest or formal charge.

Tacoronte’s death prompted protests by students in Morelos against gender-based violence. At UAEM’s Institute of Educational Sciences in Cuernavaca, classmates lit candles and left flowers at a memorial. Friends remembered Tacoronte as enthusiastic, sociable and eager to learn. Ximena Becerril, a classmate, said Tacoronte was excited about the medicinal plant fair and interested in Indigenous communities. Her family and hometown also called for accountability. The city council of Santa Brígida in Gran Canaria said Tacoronte had begun the exchange with the enthusiasm of a young woman building her future. What India’s ‘Cockroach’ protests reveal about digital authoritarianism

What happened

The Cockroach Janata Party (CJP), which began as an online joke about unemployed Indian youth, grew into a nationwide protest movement over exam leaks, unemployment and political accountability. Its success in forcing concessions also exposed how facial recognition and police databases can turn democratic participation into a lasting record of dissent.

The movement began in May 2026 after Chief Justice Surya Kant compared unemployed young people to cockroaches. Political consultant Abhijeet Dipke transformed the insult into a rallying call, and the name Cockroach Janata Party deliberately echoed the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP). Anger over alleged NEET medical entrance-exam leaks, recruitment scandals and failures in the education system broadened into a campaign over the dignity and political future of a generation.

The most serious confrontation came on July 20, when thousands of supporters attempted to march from the Jantar Mantar observatory in New Delhi toward Parliament. Police used water cannons, tear gas, pellet guns and lathis, while hundreds were detained and cases were filed. Delhi Police said people with criminal records had joined the protest and that the operation was necessary for public safety. The Supreme Court later agreed to examine allegations of excessive force.

The protest also marked a shift from visible crowd control to digital monitoring. Delhi Police deployed mobile surveillance vans, 360-degree cameras and facial-recognition technology. Its Ikshana system reportedly flagged 2,873 people as having existing criminal records. A subsequent Scroll.in investigation found that at least 25 of those identified—including people accused of murder and rape—were already in jail when the system supposedly detected them at the demonstration.

That discrepancy has intensified a legal dispute over whether facial recognition and biometric data were collected without consent and hosted by private companies. The technology had originally been acquired to identify missing and trafficked children, but was later used to monitor political gatherings. A petition challenging that use has reached the Supreme Court, while India’s legal safeguards remain fragmented.

The CJP nevertheless extracted significant concessions. Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned on July 25 after a 36-day protest over alleged examination irregularities, and the government promised not to punish demonstrators. On September 1, the Supreme Court ordered nationwide closure of FIRs linked to the July protests after the central government and four BJP-ruled states sought to quash more than 100 cases. The court was also told that families of NEET aspirants who died by suicide after the exam’s cancellation would receive compensation. The government reserved the right to file fresh FIRs against nearly 2,900 protesters with existing criminal records, but CJP co-convener Saurav Das withdrew a planned September 5 march.

Dash presents the episode as a digital-age paradox: social media enabled decentralized organizing and reportedly helped the CJP build an Instagram following exceeding 26 million, while the same infrastructure allowed the state to identify and classify participants. The movement demonstrates both the power of online mobilization and the risk that exercising the right to protest can create a searchable, durable record of political activity.

Cuba’s Official Gazette published 19 legal regulations on Tuesday to update the agricultural framework, with the stated goals of increasing food production and advancing food sovereignty. A study of 23 moderately trained young men found that smelling chocolate before and between sets of leg extensions was associated with more repetitions without a higher reported sense of effort. The dark chocolate aroma was associated with lower hunger, less desire and intention to eat, and greater fullness compared with the control and milk-chocolate conditions. The milk chocolate scent was rated as more pleasant, but it did not significantly alter appetite measures.
