What happened

Human rights conditions in Myanmar have sunk to a new low, the UN said, as a report covering June 2025 to May 2026 described unrelenting conflict, military violence and a growing “conflict economy” that is deepening civilian suffering. The UN said illegal rare-earth mining is part of that illicit economy, alongside other abuses that have left civilians facing severe hardship. In northern Rakhine, the UN accused the Arakan Army of arbitrary arrests, sexual violence, land seizures and blocking displaced Rohingya from returning, while satellite imagery showed roughly 2,000 acres seized.

What happened

More than 40 members of Congress are pressing the Trump administration to win the release of Sama Safi, a 20-year-old US citizen held by Israel since June, as lawmakers say her health is deteriorating and her case has become a test of US protection for citizens abroad.

The letter, sent to US Secretary of State Marco Rubio and led by Congresswoman Rashida Tlaib, says the Palestinian American student is being held under conditions that are worsening her condition. Six US senators, including Elizabeth Warren, Bernie Sanders and Chris Van Hollen, joined dozens of House members in signing on.

Safi was attending Birzeit University when Israeli forces took her from her home in the occupied West Bank in a predawn raid nearly three months ago, according to the account in the letter. The lawmakers said she has been held without charge and that she was among four young women detained in June as part of an Israeli crackdown on anti-occupation activism.

Lawmakers warn her condition is worsening

On Tuesday, the lawmakers said Safi’s health is deteriorating “under appalling conditions” in Israeli custody. In the letter, they wrote that “every day that Sama remains wrongfully detained in these inhumane conditions, she is at greater risk of irreversible and life-threatening repercussions.”

They added: “Her life is at risk on your watch. We urge you to act swiftly and use every tool at your disposal to secure the immediate release of Sama Safi so that she can receive the medical care she urgently needs and return to her family, her studies, and the life and freedom that every 20-year-old deserves.”

The letter says Safi has a chronic medical condition, Familial Mediterranean Fever, but has not had regular access to her medication. It also says she has been moved across several facilities and has described repeated verbal abuse, painful stress positions, rapidly spreading infectious skin disease, rats and mattresses “reeking of feces.”

Washington says it is tracking the case

The State Department said it does not comment on congressional correspondence and declined to comment on the specifics of Safi’s case, citing privacy concerns. A spokesperson said the administration of President Donald Trump “has no higher priority than the safety and security” of US citizens.

The spokesperson said the department is aware of reports of an American detained in the West Bank and is tracking the situation. When Americans are detained abroad, the department advocates for access to adequate medical care, proper treatment and regular consular visits, the spokesperson said.

Raed Jarrar, advocacy director at the US-based rights group DAWN, said Safi’s detention should trigger suspension of US military aid to Israel under Washington’s regulations, including the Leahy Law. He said, “Sama Safi is an American citizen. Her own government has abandoned her,” and argued that US law requires more than advocacy.

A broader pattern in the occupied West Bank

The lawmakers’ intervention also sits against a wider backdrop described in the same account. Over the past years, Israeli soldiers and settlers have killed several US citizens, including Sayfollah Musallet, who was beaten to death in a settler attack last year. In February, Israeli settlers fatally shot 19-year-old American citizen Nasrallah Abu Siyam. In 2024, the Israeli military killed Aysenur Ezgi Eygi, a US activist from Seattle.

None of those incidents resulted in criminal charges against the perpetrators. Additionally, Israel detained American teenager Mohammed Ibrahim for nine months last year and released him only after an outcry from US lawmakers, after his family warned that he might die from malnutrition and mistreatment in Israeli custody.

The same account cited rights groups that have documented abuses against Palestinian detainees, including sexual violence, and quoted UN special rapporteur on torture Alice Jill Edwards saying in May that emergency measures introduced after 7 October 2023 exposed detainees to torture, potentially unlawful deaths, incommunicado detention and degrading conditions.

1 in 4 former NFL players who died from 2016 to 2021 had CTE, study estimates
global_front_pagepublic_healthpolitics_and_powermaterial_harmmajor supplement
What happened

At least one in four former NFL players who died between 2016 and 2021 had chronic traumatic encephalopathy, a BMJ study estimates, in an effort to move beyond brain-donation bias and provide a clearer picture of how much CTE has affected ex-players.

CTE is a neurodegenerative disease associated with repeated blows to the head. It can only be diagnosed after death, even though symptoms may include memory loss, impulse control problems, anxiety, depression and anger issues. It is marked in the brain by an abnormal buildup of the protein tau.

How the new estimate was built

The study was designed to address a limitation in a separate recent analysis that found CTE in 99% of former NFL players who had donated their brains for research. That earlier work could be biased, the new paper said, because it relied on donations from families who may have noticed symptoms while the person was still alive.

To get around that problem, researchers from Mass General Brigham, Boston University and the Concussion & CTE Foundation — many of whom had also been involved in earlier CTE research — examined the total number of NFL deaths between 2008 and 2021. They compared death certificate data with the number of brain donors with confirmed CTE, then narrowed the window to 2016 through 2021 because that period had the highest number of brain donations.

Among 878 former players who died in that time frame, 235 had donated their brains for research, and 215 of those donors had been diagnosed with CTE. Many of the donations were analyzed at brain banks at Boston University and the University of California, San Francisco.

Why the finding matters

An editorial published with the study called it “one of the most informative estimates to date of CTE burden among former NFL players.” The research also sits alongside a broader body of evidence: CTE is most often seen in people who play contact sports such as football, hockey and soccer, but it has also been detected in the brains of troops injured in explosions and in some people who experience domestic violence.

The new paper does not resolve the question of symptoms. Rachel Grashow, director of research initiatives for the Football Players Health Study at Harvard University, said the study is important, but noted that a diagnosis of CTE does not always correlate with symptoms. She said the scientific community would see the paper as useful because “we’ve got a denominator,” but worried that others might mistake the number for a symptom count.

Daniel Daneshvar, the study’s lead author and chair of physical medicine and rehabilitation at Mass General Brigham, said repeated traumatic brain injuries are a known factor in elevated rates of neurodegenerative disease. He added that in NFL players, there is “about a four times higher rate of death from neurodegenerative disease than the general population.”

An NFL spokesperson said Tuesday that the league “continuously strives to make the game of football safer, including by implementing strategies to reduce concussions and head impacts,” and said it remains committed to expanding resources for the NFL community.

Daneshvar also said the biggest share of head impacts in these former players did not happen in the NFL itself. “The bulk of the head impacts that these former NFL players experienced didn’t occur at the NFL level,” he said. “They occurred at the collegiate level … and at the high school level and in many cases the youth level.”

Joburg’s council housing system crumbles into chaos
global_south_and_exploitedpolitics_and_powereconomy_and_businessregional expansion
What happened

Johannesburg’s council-owned rental housing stock is sliding deeper into disrepair, with hijacked buildings, unpaid rent and thousands of idle units leaving residents stuck on waiting lists and forcing the city to defend a system it now says is financially unsustainable.

The City’s Department of Human Settlements manages 21,909 potential units or beds, but only 10,584 are billable, according to city spokesperson Nthatisi Modingoane. In 2024/25, billed rental income was R117.45-million, while arrears stood at R126.70-million. The city says that means non-payment now exceeds annual billed revenue.

Hijacked buildings and a shrinking rental base

The city says 15 of its residential properties have been lost to illegal occupiers and hijacked by criminal syndicates, part of a broader pattern of 40 hijacked buildings identified in the inner city. Modingoane said the 15 city-owned hijacked buildings are being processed through the “bad buildings strategy” and could later be released to Joshco, the Johannesburg Social Housing Company, for conversion into social housing and affordable rental stock once reclaimed and rehabilitated.

Behind that, the maintenance problem is widening. The city says the total maintenance and restoration backlog is R1.5-billion, while only R70-million is allocated annually. Modingoane said R300-million a year would be needed to keep the portfolio habitable and prevent further decay. He said the rental housing portfolio is unsustainable because high operating costs and low collection rates do not cover maintenance and operating expenses.

Living with decay and no security

In South Hills, a resident who asked not to be named said the building had not been maintained for years. She said residents do some of their own repairs, including fixing lights, geysers, stoves and paint, and that they get free water and electricity, but refuse removal is scarce and the block is full of rats and cockroaches.

Another South Hills resident said gangs had taken over some blocks in her council housing complex. She said people were being killed by gang members trying to take over occupied flats and that there was no security to stop it. Occupants, many of whom live in appalling conditions, admitted they had not paid rent for years and would not give their names because they feared gang members who occupy some flats.

The city’s position is that it will not carry out repairs because of non-payment, leaving the council and tenants locked in a stalemate while the buildings deteriorate further. The result is pooled sewage in courtyards, months of uncollected garbage and decaying utility systems.

What the city says it will do next

The city has set refurbishment targets for 2025/26: five public hostels, 10 blocks of flats and four senior citizen facilities are slated for significant upgrading. It also says Joshco plans to add at least 5,000 rental units over the next five years through turnkey projects and the conversion of reclaimed inner-city buildings.

Ward 57 councillor Faeeza Chame, whose ward includes several council housing blocks, said the city has lost control of its housing units. She said some better blocks are being kept reasonably clean by residents themselves, but hundreds of units are standing empty and unfit for occupation. Her view was that if the city repaired more blocks and took control, it could house hundreds of people who have been waiting for years.

Brazil fines TikTok $30m for child data privacy violations
global_south_and_exploitedpolitics_and_poweranchor
What happened

Brazil’s data protection authority has fined TikTok’s parent company, ByteDance, nearly $30 million over alleged mishandling of children’s and teenagers’ personal data, ordering the company to delete illegally collected data and strengthen youth protections while giving it 10 days to appeal.

In its ruling, the regulator said TikTok breached data privacy laws in both logged-in accounts and guest browsing sessions by collecting and processing young users’ personal information without a valid legal basis or adequate safeguards. It estimated that TikTok may have processed the data of at least 8 million children and said the platform showed systemic deficiencies in its age-verification mechanism. The order also requires TikTok to erase all data amassed illegally and to formulate an improved comprehensive youth-protection framework. The authority said TikTok’s “logged-out feed,” which lets people browse without creating an account, enables minors to use the platform while bypassing age-verification checks. According to the regulator, that feature is available in Brazil but not in the United States or Europe, where access is limited to registered users.

Earlier this month, the same regulator ordered Discord to suspend its livestreaming feature across Brazil, accusing the platform of failing to protect children and teenagers. Authorities linked that move to the death of a 13-year-old girl after a livestream in which they say she was encouraged to harm herself.

The TikTok ruling sits within a broader push by Brazil’s government under President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and the Supreme Court to regulate internet use by minors, with mixed results so far. Similar efforts are also underway in Australia, Canada and Indonesia.

What happened

The CPC Central Committee Political Bureau set out a broad economic work agenda on July 30, calling for stronger policy support, more domestic demand and tighter risk control as China looks to secure a good start to the 15th Five-Year Plan period (2026-30).

The meeting said economic work in the second half of the year should “thoroughly implement” CPC Central Committee decisions, intensify counter-cyclical adjustment, and bring forward practical incremental policies to boost momentum and social vitality. It called for a more proactive fiscal policy and a moderately loose monetary policy, while saying macroeconomic policies should improve efficiency.

On the fiscal side, the meeting said the average daily balance of treasury cash remained relatively high nationwide in the first half of the year, reflecting ample fiscal funds. It urged faster execution of fiscal spending and bond-fund use so they can be turned into physical work sooner, along with stronger monitoring of fiscal operations and treasury cash scheduling to safeguard the grassroots “three guarantees” of basic living needs, salaries and government functioning.

Monetary policy was also given a central role. The meeting called for timely use and adjustment of monetary tools, adequate liquidity, smoother transmission of policy, and lower social financing costs. It said structural monetary tools should support domestic demand, scientific and technological innovation, and micro, small and medium-sized enterprises, while fiscal and financial policies should work together to promote domestic demand.

The agenda then turned to demand and investment. It said some commodity consumption sectors have reached saturation or a plateau, so policy should adapt to different consumer groups, expand quality supply and cultivate new consumption. Service consumption was described as a key future area. With fixed-asset investment down 5.7 percent in the first half of the year, the meeting called for stabilizing investment, advancing the “six major networks,” and accelerating major projects in the 15th Five-Year Plan. It said 800 billion yuan in policy-backed financial instruments had been put in place this year, mainly to support major projects.

Innovation, markets and risk control

The meeting also said China should accelerate a modernized industrial system through innovation, including more support for basic research, deeper integration of scientific and technological innovation with industrial innovation, and the “AI+” initiative. It called for more open and orderly markets, better rules for a unified national market, and continued efforts to address “involutionary” competition and overdue payments.

Other priorities included expanding opening-up and trade, strengthening rural development and food security, improving livelihoods, and guarding against risks in areas including real estate, local government debt and finance. The meeting ended by saying China’s economy has room for maneuver, market potential and resilience, and that the goal is steady and sustained high-quality development.

What happened

A study mapping cell types in the adult human hippocampus found that people with depression seemed to produce new neurons less effectively than controls, but it does not show that the impairment causes depression.

The paper, published last week in *Nature Medicine*, builds on more than 20 years of suspicion that disrupted neurogenesis — the process of making new neurons — could be involved in depression. Amelia Eisch, a neuroscientist at the University of Pennsylvania who was not involved, said it was the first study to show at single-cell resolution where the process appears to go wrong and what molecular machinery is involved.

The researchers analysed brain tissue collected shortly after death from 30 people. Eleven had major depressive disorder, also called clinical depression, and 19 had no mental-health conditions. They sequenced RNA in the nuclei of almost 500,000 hippocampal cells, using gene activity to infer stages of neuronal development.

That analysis suggested a stalled trajectory: compared with controls, brains from people with depression had a greater proportion of neural stem cells and a lower proportion of neuroblasts, the precursors to neurons. Maura Boldrini Dupont, one of the authors at Columbia University, said the team found “several potentially druggable pathways” involved in the pathogenesis.

The results also add weight to the idea that the human brain keeps making new neurons into adulthood, despite long-standing debate over adult neurogenesis. Gerd Kempermann of the German Center for Neurodegenerative Diseases said the study goes further by suggesting the newly generated neurons may also have a distinct function.

Still, the work does not establish that impaired neurogenesis helps cause depression, and the authors note that depression involves multiple brain areas, not just the hippocampus. Evgenia Salta of the Netherlands Institute for Neuroscience pointed out that the study did not find fewer immature neurons in people with depression, which keeps open the question of exactly when the process becomes affected.

What happened

NASA scientists say some microbes carried into space by human explorers could survive in sheltered, shadowed areas near the Moon’s South Pole, raising fresh concerns about contamination as crews and robots move into those regions and about how future Mars life searches will be protected from Earth-born material.

They examined five organisms, including *Aspergillus niger*, *Bacillus subtilis*, *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Deinococcus radiodurans* and several *Fusarium* species. NASA can reduce contamination on robotic spacecraft by heating them above 400 degrees Fahrenheit, but that approach is not possible for crewed missions, making lunar contamination harder to manage. The researchers said surviving is not the same as thriving: there is no evidence the Moon offers the liquid water and moderate temperatures microbes would need to grow.

Newton’s 300-year-old law just passed its biggest test yet
science_generalglobal_south_and_exploitedpolitics_and_powerscience anchorscience_journalism
What happened

Researchers using the Atacama Cosmology Telescope have put gravity to its largest cosmic test yet, and the result strengthens Newton’s inverse-square law across galaxy-cluster scales while narrowing room for modified-gravity explanations.

For decades, astronomers have seen stars and galaxies moving faster than their visible mass seems able to explain. University of Pennsylvania researcher Patricio A. Gallardo said that leaves two broad possibilities: the universe contains unseen dark matter, or the fundamental equations for gravity need to be changed.

Ancient light, vast distances

Gallardo and collaborators used observations of the cosmic microwave background, the ancient light released about 380,000 years after the Big Bang, to study how galaxy clusters affect that radiation as it passes through them. By examining those signatures across hundreds of thousands of galaxy clusters separated by tens of millions of light years, the team tested gravity on scales far beyond the Solar System.

The telescope, which is roughly three to four stories tall and was developed largely by Penn researchers led by Mark Devlin, let the group compare how strongly gravity acts across enormous distances. The result, published in *Physical Review Letters*, was that gravity decreases with distance almost exactly as Newton’s equations predict and as Einstein’s theory of general relativity incorporates.

“It is remarkable that the law of the inverse of the squares... is still holding its ground in the 21st century,” Gallardo said.

That matters because the same mismatch has long shown up in two places: stars orbiting in the outer parts of galaxies move much faster than visible matter seems able to support, and galaxies in clusters also move at speeds that do not fit the observed mass alone. Gallardo said the measurements do not support the idea that changing gravity explains the missing mass. Instead, they strengthen the case that an unseen component, dark matter, supplies the extra pull.

The finding also supports one of the foundations of modern cosmology and sharply limits alternatives such as Modified Newtonian Dynamics, or MOND, which try to account for unusual cosmic motions by changing gravity itself. Gallardo said the study strengthens the evidence that the universe contains dark matter, but it still does not show what dark matter is made of.

Future measurements of the cosmic microwave background, along with larger galaxy surveys, are expected to test gravity with even greater precision.
