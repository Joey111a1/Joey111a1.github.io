What happened

Pat McFadden used a visit to Boots in Nottingham to argue that work should be part of the response to young people’s mental health problems, as Labour’s youth jobs guarantee begins to place some of the first subsidised recruits in jobs and the government weighs how far the scheme can go.

First beneficiaries at Boots

The work and pensions secretary was speaking on Wednesday at Boots’s headquarters in Nottingham, where several 18- to 21-year-olds had been taken on under the guarantee, with their wages paid by the taxpayer for six months. Some were packing and labelling boxes to be posted to households, while others were doing administrative work.

The young workers told McFadden about long periods of fruitless jobhunting. He said it was “amazing” to hear their stories and described them as young people who had been out of work for “one, two, up to four years”. He added that most had been in the jobs for only a few weeks, but that their confidence and self-belief already seemed to be improving.

Why the government says work matters

A rise in mental illness is widely cited as one reason for the increase in the number of young people not in education, employment or training, or Neets, in recent years. McFadden said the relationship could run the other way as well.

“Work is part of the answer. Work is good for you,” he said, adding that it was bad for mental health to be “sitting at home, isolated”. He said he saw the issue not only as economic, but as a response to greater mental health problems among young people, and said the last thing the government wanted was for a diagnosis to lead to a life on benefits.

At a roundtable discussion, he asked the young workers how they felt about moving into paid work. They pointed to better social lives and clearer daily routines. McFadden also said, “We mustn’t blame young people for the challenges they are facing in the labour market.”

The wider policy test

The government’s jobs guarantee is aimed eventually at 18- to 24-year-olds in Great Britain who have been claiming universal credit and seeking work for at least 18 months. It is intended to create 90,000 subsidised placements over three years and is being piloted in six areas with high unemployment, including central and east Scotland, Birmingham and Solihull, and south-east and south-west Wales. The government wants it to be nationwide by November.

The broader backdrop is a growing number of young people outside work or education. The number of Neets in the UK recently topped 1 million, with the latest data due out on Thursday. Alan Milburn, who is reviewing youth inactivity for the government, has warned that the figure could reach 1.25 million by 2030 without intervention, and his final report is due in the autumn.

McFadden said welfare reform would probably be part of the response. He criticised what he called the “black and white” distinction in the universal credit system, saying it too readily split people into “fit for work” and “unfit for work” and left some without support or conditions. He called that the culture of “signed off and written off” and said it needed to change.

He also highlighted a recent initiative asking claimants with no obligation to look for work to attend voluntary interviews with work coaches at their local Jobcentre. Earlier in the day, he had been at a Sainsbury’s branch in London with the prime minister, Andy Burnham and the London mayor, Sadiq Khan, where they were highlighting 10,000 internships for young people. McFadden praised Boots and Sainsbury’s and urged other big employers to join what he called a “national cause” of tackling youth unemployment.

Death toll rises from Himalayan flood between Nepal and Tibet as hundreds remain missing
china_domesticglobal_south_and_exploitedpublic_healthmaterial_harmanchor
What happened

A violent flood and landslides in the Gyirong mountain corridor between Nepal and Tibet have killed at least 160 people and left more than 700 missing, many of them foreign travelers, while authorities still disagree on how the disaster began.

The toll keeps rising

At least 157 people died in Nepal and three in Tibet after the flood hit on Wednesday morning, around 10:30 a.m. China time, in the border corridor used by mountaineers. Nepal police said they had recovered 157 bodies from the disaster zone.

The scale of the missing list is still growing. Nepal’s Tourism Board said 384 travelers are on a provisional list of people not yet located — 291 foreigners and 93 nationals — and that list is being added to by almost 100 members of the security forces and 60 workers from different hydropower projects. Chinese authorities have also counted at least 265 missing, according to the first balance released by state broadcaster CCTV, taking the total to more than 700 missing.

Security-camera footage from the area shows how quickly the disaster unfolded: people running as water and mud swept away buses, and destroyed buildings, bridges and other infrastructure in seconds.

Who is missing remains unclear

The provisional lists include many foreign nationals. EFE, citing Nepalese sources, said the missing include 133 Indian citizens, 19 Malaysians, 33 Britons, 47 Americans, 34 Australians, 24 Canadians, seven Ukrainians, six Singaporeans, six South Africans and five Japanese.

Those lists are still being checked, and local authorities say it is not yet possible to conclude that all of those people were swept away by the flood. The question of whether Spanish nationals were caught up in the disaster is also unsettled: Foreign Ministry sources said there were no Spaniards affected, but EFE, citing Nepalese authorities, reported that two Spaniards could not be located.

The high number of foreigners reflects the role of this corridor between Kathmandu and Tibet. It is used by organized groups after another crossing was destroyed in the 2015 earthquake, and it serves routes to Lhasa, Everest, Mount Kailash and Lake Manasarovar. On the Nepal side, Rasuwa also gives access to the Langtang valley hiking routes.

Search crews are still being held back

What caused the flood is still not clear, and the point where it started is also unknown. One hypothesis gaining strength is that it may have been triggered by the draining of a glacial lake. Nepal’s hydrology department is still investigating that possibility.

Other explanations have been offered, but only as preliminary accounts. Nepal’s foreign minister told parliament that initial information suggested an earthquake caused a large landslide that blocked the Bhote Koshi River, which then overflowed and triggered the flood. The U.S. Geological Survey recorded a magnitude 5.2 tremor in Tibetan territory at that time, but later said the seismic activity was actually due to a landslide caused by the force of the water. China’s Ministry of Natural Resources, according to CCTV, has also pointed to a possible avalanche in the Donglin Sangro basin on the Nepal side, but described that as a preliminary hypothesis.

The rescue effort is being hampered by the terrain and the damage. Nepal has deployed the army, police, medical teams and helicopters, and has told people living along riverbanks to move to higher ground while extending warnings downstream. Roads, communications and electricity were cut in the remote Himalayan area, and the Ministry of Energy said around 430 megawatts of generating capacity were affected.

On the Chinese side, CCTV said 601 rescuers and 113 vehicles were taking part in the operation, but by around 8 p.m. local time the teams were still 1.5 kilometers from Gyirong, blocked by about 1.5 meters of mud. The images and the surviving accounts have made clear how fast the flood moved: Bibek Kumal, a 24-year-old Nepalese laborer, said a “wall of water” hit him while he was digging a latrine pit in Bidur, and he survived only by clinging to a mango tree after his four coworkers disappeared.

What happened

Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers agreed to the proposal this morning and congratulated both sides, as Meta avoided any admission of guilt.

The cost of ending the trial

Meta will pay more than $12 billion under one account of the deal, and other reports put the settlement at up to $18 billion. However the final figure is described, it is far below the roughly $200 billion initially sought and only a fraction of the $60 billion Meta made in profit last year.

The money can be used by the states and other parties to cover litigation costs and to support school and community programs. Brian Schwalb, the attorney general of the District of Columbia, called the settlement a “monumental public health victory” that will “fundamentally and immediately change how young people use Instagram and Facebook.”

Meta framed the agreement as part of its “longstanding efforts to empower parents and support teens,” and it did not admit wrongdoing. The company still faces numerous lawsuits filed by individuals and school districts.

What changes for teens on Facebook and Instagram

The settlement requires Meta to make a series of design changes to its social-media platforms. Teen users, by default, will not have access to most features of Instagram and Facebook at night, though they can still access messages and settings. They also will not receive notifications during school hours, defined as 8 a.m. to 3 p.m. on weekdays from August 15 to June 15.

Meta will also limit teen users to a maximum of one hour a day per platform. The agreement does not count time spent sending messages or consuming long-form content, which it defines as audio or video at least 22 minutes long. Parents can still manually change these settings for their children.

The company must interrupt scrolling every 15 minutes with pop-ups encouraging “productive pauses,” then study how well those interruptions work and submit the findings to an auditor. Meta has also agreed to regular auditing of its age-verification systems and of its efforts to stop minors from opening multiple accounts to get around the restrictions.

Other changes are aimed at what the settlement describes as the most problematic parts of social media’s design, including short-form video and the “never-ending feed.”

A test case for the rest of the industry

Meta is presenting the deal as a new standard, and it is urging TikTok and YouTube to adopt similar rules. The company has said the protections will only be truly effective if its rivals follow suit, and California Attorney General Rob Bonta said the settlement is meant to push broader industry reform.

Whether that happens is still unresolved, but the agreement already marks a major shift in how Meta’s platforms are supposed to work for minors in the United States. It is also one of the biggest child-safety settlements a tech company has faced, and the states that brought the case are treating it as a template for broader changes beyond Meta itself.

Treasury notes mature in two to 10 years, while Treasury bonds mature in 20 or 30 years, and both pay fixed interest every six months. But the yield is not the same as that coupon payment: it is the annualised return an investor expects from buying the bond at its current market price and holding it to maturity. That is what has been happening. Yields on longer-term US Treasuries have risen sharply in recent weeks, with the 30-year bond going above 5.33 per cent on Aug 18, its highest level since 2007. The rise has been driven by a mix of immediate inflation worries and longer-running concern about how much US debt the market must absorb. The war with Iran pushed oil prices higher and fuelled fears that inflation could stay elevated, while worries about the US fiscal position added pressure. As one strategist cited by CNA put it, Washington’s large deficits mean the Treasury must keep issuing substantial debt to fund spending and refinance maturing obligations. Higher US yields usually push up Asian bond yields and borrowing costs too. That can mean more expensive mortgages and business loans, and it can also dampen investment if financing becomes costlier. What happened

Researchers introduced CyberFactory, a unified open-source framework for cybersecurity training that connects data construction, trajectory synthesis, and model training across proof-of-concept generation, vulnerability patching, and cybersecurity question answering. It turns public vulnerability artifacts, including CVEs from the wild, into executable and verifiable task instances, and uses a reusable vulnerability-analysis skill to guide source inspection, problem solving with domain prior, and evidence-based validation. The resulting supervision is agentic: the model works with tools and target environments and revises its solutions based on execution feedback.

Using these trajectories, the team trained and released Aegis, which internalizes the skill-guided procedure without needing the skill at inference time. On CyberGym, Aegis reached 52.4% Pass@1 under a one-hour budget, improving on its Qwen 3.5 base model by 22.8 points and outperforming the evaluated general-purpose backbones under the same scaffold.

What happened

Recuris, a recursive Experiential-Working Memory architecture for long-horizon agent harnesses, was introduced to address recursive self-improvement in tasks where growing histories obscure task state and can misalign skill invocation. Across four long-horizon benchmarks and ten models, it improved task success in 35 of 37 completed model-benchmark pairs. The preprint reports gains including +17.8 points for GPT-5.6 Sol and +15.6 for Claude Opus 5 on tau-bench, bringing Opus 5 to 87.9%, and +16.6 and +13.5 points for Qwen3.6-27B and Qwen3.6-35B on SkillFlow.

The architecture uses Working Memory to track task progress and guide skill selection from Experiential Memory, aiming to ground skill use in current needs rather than the full history. The same execution then becomes structured evidence for a fixed Meta-Agent, which turns it into localized, validation-gated updates to Skill Memory in a bounded recursive memory-evolution loop. The advantage widens on longer interactions, reaching +32.2 points on the longest tasks, while common long-horizon failures fall by up to 80%.

Automated prototyping of genetic codes
science_generalglobal_south_and_exploitedpublic_healthscience_cs_aiscience anchorscience_journalism
What happened

A new automated workflow, AGENTEX, is letting researchers prototype alternative genetic codes in cell-free translation systems, testing whether tRNAs with altered 3′ ends can be charged and decoded without derailing translation.

The work starts from the standard genetic code, in which 64 codons encode 20 canonical amino acids. New-to-nature genetic codes have been proposed for new chemistries, therapeutics and ecosystem engineering, but recoding an organism’s genome remains exceptionally difficult. The researchers focused on a built-in ribosomal gatekeeper: two Watson–Crick interactions in the large subunit that recognize the 3′ CCA end of tRNAs and exclude tRNAs with alternative 3′ sequences.

What the system showed

To see whether non-CCA tRNAs could still be used in new codes, the team studied non-CCA-3′ tRNAs, or otRNAs, and whether natural aminoacyl tRNA synthetases would charge them. They developed multiplexed, automated methods to read aminoacylation in libraries of synthetic tRNAs and found that the tRNA 3′ end was far more flexible than expected. Most otRNAs were aminoacylated by all Escherichia coli aaRSs.

In a benchmark using PURExpress Δ (aa, tRNA), about 62% of CGA-ending otRNAs were charged, along with about 57% of CUA-ending otRNAs and about 42% of CAA-ending otRNAs. Building on that result, the researchers developed cell-free translation systems that enabled compressed genetic codes of 34 aaRSs for 34 codons.

AGENTEX then tied the pieces together in a robotic workflow. Using custom Python code and an Opentrons OT-2 system, the team automated steps from tRNA production through translation and purification. They also built a high-sensitivity luminescence reporter assay and used LC-MS/MS to check peptide products and translational crosstalk. With AGENTEX, they evaluated two genetic codes alongside the standard genetic code and saw non-standard amino acid incorporation and reassignment of up to three codons.

The researchers say the findings matter because they point toward radically new translation systems and the synthesis of biopolymers with multiple non-standard monomers. They also argue the work helps frame what past and future genetic codes might be possible, while showing that a cell-free, automated setup can rapidly test designs that are difficult to build directly in genomes.

Ultrafast and reference-free sequence discovery in single-cell data
science_generalpublic_healthscience_cs_aieconomy_and_businessscience anchorscience_journalism
What happened

Malva gives researchers a way to search raw RNA sequence across massive single-cell and spatial transcriptomics datasets without a reference genome, opening queries that standard pipelines cannot handle and turning cell atlases into sequence-resolved resources instead of only gene-count tables.

Why single-cell data has been hard to search

The problem starts with scale and with what existing pipelines keep. Single-cell and spatial transcriptomics now generate petabyte-scale profiles, but standard workflows are reference-centric: they align reads to a user-provided reference and usually retain gene or isoform counts rather than the raw sequences themselves. That makes it difficult to search these datasets for a particular RNA sequence, mutation, splice junction, pathogen, or transcript location without collecting, downloading, and reprocessing huge numbers of files.

The paper frames this as a gap in the way public single-cell resources are used. The Human Cell Atlas and related efforts quantify RNA across healthy cells, disease samples, organoids and other systems, but the raw sequence space has remained hard to interrogate at scale. Malva is presented as a platform built to fill that gap.

How Malva works

Malva is described as an ultrafast, species-agnostic, reference-free computational platform for interrogating raw sequence space. It is designed to let users search for any sequence, mutation, splice junction or pathogen, and even for the spatial location of arbitrary transcripts. The platform’s continuously expanding Malva Index currently comprises around 74 million cells from thousands of experiments in health and disease.

The method adapts the k-mer indexing principle used in bulk sequence search to single-cell data. Each read is broken into k-mers and stored with the cell barcode of origin, which allows constant-time lookups without alignment. To make the system scalable, Malva uses non-overlapping k-mer sampling, sparse inverted lists that store only expressing cell identifiers, and independent per-sample indexing with incremental merging. The paper says this lets the index expand continuously while keeping queries memory efficient.

Malva also works as a discovery layer rather than only a search tool. The platform can perform reference-free cell-type clustering and de novo assembly of cell-type-specific sequences, and it can connect to state-of-the-art neural networks for more complex searches and automated analyses. The paper says that, by doing this, Malva moves single-cell atlases beyond static gene counts toward dynamic, sequence-resolved resources.

What the platform can already do

The reported benchmarks emphasize speed. On a Stereo-seq mouse liver atlas with about 61 billion reads across 20 sections, Malva completed indexing in 24 CPU hours, using 8 GB of peak memory. Search latency was reported at 70 ms for a single 24-mer, 0.9 seconds for a 1 kb transcript and about 1 minute for 1,000 transcripts on a single CPU core.

The platform was also tested on larger collections. The paper says a large collection of about 1.5 TB of raw data was reindexed, and that Malva outperformed the compared methods on indexing speed while keeping strong query throughput. It also reports robustness to sequencing errors, with sensitivity, specificity, pseudocount correlations and downstream analyses remaining stable up to error rates of 2% and degrading gradually at higher rates.

Malva’s searches are not limited to straightforward gene lookup. The paper says researchers can identify cell types and predict cell–cell similarity directly from sequence composition. It also describes searches for SNPs, isoforms, circRNAs and somatic mutations, including an analysis of 296,985 common SNP probes, detection of CDR1as in excitatory neurons, and pan-cancer mutation querying using the scTML database of 280 tumour samples across 16 cancer types.

The platform does not replace reference-based quantification pipelines. Instead, the paper says it extends them by enabling sequence-level queries that are otherwise inaccessible at very large scale, including in cases where references are not readily available. It also notes a limitation of practical use: Malva currently supports queries of at least 24 nucleotides and uses exact k-mer matching, and single-cell sequence search can reveal donor-specific genotype information that should be treated with the same care as raw genome or exome data.

Tidal tomography reveals a thermal anomaly beneath Mars’s crustal dichotomy
science_generalglobal_south_and_exploitedpolitics_and_powerclimate_disastereconomy_and_businessscience anchorscience_journalism
What happened

Seasonal tidal forcing on Mars has been used to probe the planet’s deep interior, and spacecraft tracking now points to a warm anomaly beneath the southern highlands that may help explain the long-debated crustal dichotomy between Mars’s northern lowlands and southern highlands.

Researchers analyzed Earth-based X-band Doppler tracking data from the Mars Global Surveyor, Mars Odyssey and Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter spacecraft to recover seasonal, time-variable gravity signals. They found that degree-3 components of Mars’s gravity field differ by up to 300% from what would be expected for a spherically symmetric planet. The deviations are consistent with a north–south change in the mantle’s effective shear modulus of more than 20%, in a pattern that closely matches the surface expression of the crustal dichotomy.

On that basis, the study infers a present-day thermal anomaly of about 200–400 °C in the mantle beneath the southern highlands. The authors say the temperature variation could reflect regional mantle convection or insulation by the thick southern highlands crust, which has persisted for several billion years. They also note that the hemispheric structure is tied to a broader Martian contrast: the north is dominated by low-lying plains, while the south is higher, rougher and more heavily cratered.

The dichotomy has long been linked to other hemispheric differences, including an estimated 25 km variation in crustal thickness, differences in crustal density, magnetization and seismic wave attenuation. Because the northern lowlands may once have held surface water before Mars’s putative oceans were lost to space, understanding how the dichotomy formed also bears on the planet’s ancient hydrology and the timing of surface conditions that could have supported life.

What happened

CrSb has been mapped in bulk as a three-dimensional g-wave altermagnet, with magnetic quantum oscillations revealing spin-split Fermi sheets whose symmetry changes with field direction and a momentum-space pattern that matches a real spherical harmonic tied to g-orbital-like order.

Bulk mapping of the order parameter

The work uses magnetic quantum oscillation measurements to probe the exchange splitting between up- and down-spin states in CrSb. By rotating a magnetic field through high- and low-symmetry directions of the CrSb Brillouin zone, the researchers report a high-resolution, bulk-sensitive 3D map of the material’s order parameter.

That matters because order parameters are what distinguish electronic phases such as magnetism and superconductivity. In this framework, ferromagnets have uniform magnetization, conventional superconductors have an isotropic gap, and unconventional systems can carry nodal symmetry. The study places CrSb in the newer altermagnet category, where up- and down-spin species have non-degenerate Fermi surfaces.

The reported data show that away from nodal orientations, the altermagnetic band structure reduces the symmetry of each spin-split Fermi sheet. In momentum space, the exchange splitting follows the profile of the u2113 = 4, m = -3 real spherical harmonic, written here as u1d4cYu2084u207bu00b3 = yz(3xu00b2-yu00b2), described as analogous to a g-orbital of the hydrogen atom. The authors say this establishes CrSb as a prototypical g-wave metallic altermagnet.

CrSb is described as a hexagonal NiAs-type compound with chromium moments alternating along the c-axis below an ordering temperature of around 740 K. The trigonal arrangement of antimony requires a 6u2083 screw rotation to map a spin-up chromium site to its spin-down counterpart, a symmetry pattern the paper connects to the proposed altermagnetic state.

What the measurements showed.

The measurements were performed with the de Haasu2013van Alphen effect. For fields aligned along nodal directions, the oscillation spectrum showed a single frequency peak; after small rotations away from those directions, the peak split into two distinct branches, consistent with non-degenerate spin-up and spin-down Fermi sheets. The study reports that this splitting was especially clear in the dogbone-like Fermi surface, while a more complex web-like sheet produced lower-frequency structure.

The authors also note that, although some altermagnets have been predicted to show a distinct frequency splitting at a field-induced Lifshitz transition, they measured up to 64 T and observed no such signature in CrSb.

Cell-type-specific eQTLs underlie the genetic architecture of complex traits
science_generalglobal_south_and_exploitedpublic_healthscience_cs_aiscience anchorscience_journalism
What happened

Cell-type-specific eQTLs appear to account for more of the genetic architecture of complex traits than shared eQTLs, according to a study that used single-cell RNA sequencing from peripheral blood mononuclear cells and addressed why bulk-tissue studies have explained only part of the signal.

The work starts from a gap in genetic research: studies of expression quantitative trait loci, or eQTLs, have identified thousands of gene-regulating variants, but the known eQTLs explain only about 10–30% of genetic effects on complex traits. The authors say previous studies had not effectively captured cell-type-specific eQTLs, even though those effects are likely to matter for complex traits. To get at that missing layer, they applied a variance component model to population-scale single-cell RNA-seq data rather than relying on bulk tissue averages.

What the single-cell analysis found

Using peripheral blood mononuclear cells from the OneK1K cohort, the study found that cell-type-specific eQTLs enrich for complex trait heritability, while cell-type-shared eQTLs did not show the same pattern. The authors also report that eQTL specificity is linked to genes with greater selective constraint, enhancer complexity and gene network connectivity, three features they say are enriched in complex traits relative to known eQTLs.

The pattern differed by eQTL class. Across the transcriptome, trans eQTLs were mostly cell-type-specific, with 60% specific, whereas cis eQTLs were mostly shared, with 30% specific. The authors say they detected cell-type-specific eQTLs for 193 genes.

To check the result, they used a second single-cell RNA-seq dataset and say the findings replicated and were consistent across ancestries. On that basis, they conclude that eQTL cell-type specificity is a key feature of gene regulation and helps explain why known eQTLs are depleted in gene regulatory effects on complex traits.

The study frames cell type as one of the contexts that bulk RNA-seq can miss. By separating cell-type-shared from cell-type-specific effects, it argues that some of the genetic influence on complex traits is being hidden in the mixture of cells rather than absent from the genome.

Insights into longevity and virus-driven adaptation from Myotis bat genomes
science_generalpublic_healthclimate_disasterscience_cs_aieconomy_and_businessscience anchorscience_journalism
What happened

Researchers assembled near-complete genomes and cell lines from eight closely related *Myotis* bat species to probe why this genus combines unusual longevity with viral tolerance, immune defence and cancer resistance, and to see whether those traits share common genetic roots.

The work focuses on *Myotis*, one of the largest bat clades, with over 139 described species across 6 continents and lifespan differences that range from the long-lived *Myotis brandtii* at 42 years to the short-lived *Myotis nigricans* at 7 years. To study how those differences evolved, the team collected skin punches, derived primary cell lines from several North American species and generated de novo, haplotype-resolved, chromosome-scale genome assemblies using long-read PacBio HiFi sequencing and Hi-C scaffolding. The eight genomes were highly contiguous, with an average of 98.6% of nucleotides assembled and average BUSCO scores of 98.2% to 98.5%.

Using genome-wide screens of positive selection, structural-variation analyses and functional experiments in primary cells, the researchers identified patterns of adaptation that they say contribute to longevity, cancer resistance and viral interactions. They report that bats show distinct modes of adaptation to DNA and RNA viruses compared with all other mammals: there was genome-wide over-representation of positive selection in DNA-virus-interacting proteins, and elevated copy-number variation in RNA-virus-interacting proteins. The study also characterizes *Myotis*-specific duplications of the immune factor EIF2AK2, also known as PKR, and says these duplications include multiple ancient segregating trans-species copy-number polymorphisms.

The longevity signal was linked to cancer biology as well. The researchers say the recurrent evolution of longevity in *Myotis* is associated with positive selection in cancer pathways, and they observed a unique response to DNA damage in primary cells of the long-lived *Myotis lucifugus*. Taken together, the results suggest that bats’ longevity and immunity are connected through pleiotropic adaptations to viruses and ageing-related disease.
