A night when missiles got through

The attack was notable not just for the scale of the damage, but for what Ukrainian defenses could not stop. One account said Russia fired more than two dozen ballistic and anti-ship missiles, and Ukrainian defenses intercepted none of them. Authorities said at least 17 people were killed and dozens wounded in Kyiv and surrounding areas.

That failure to stop the missiles landed in the middle of an air war that has increasingly put cities under pressure far from the front lines. The strikes came after Ukraine had already warned that it was running perilously low on the interceptor missiles used by Patriot systems, the defenses seen as key against some advanced ballistic missiles. Without those interceptors, Ukraine’s skies were described as “wide open” and civilians “fully exposed” to Russian missile barrages.

Warehouses, rail sites and factories were hit

The immediate damage was concentrated around civilian business and logistics sites. Ukrainian officials said the overnight strikes mainly targeted warehouses, with some hits on infrastructure and a railway station. A local official said eight people were killed in a strike on a railway station on the eastern edge of Kyiv. Other reported damage included a brewery, hardware store warehouses and industrial buildings in the Kyiv region.

Prominent Ukrainian businesses also reported losses. Iryna Chechotkina, co-owner of Rozetka, said three ballistic missiles struck a warehouse complex. Zelensky links the deaths to interceptor shortages

Volodymyr Zelensky used the aftermath to press the case for more air-defense support. He said delays in deliveries, or reluctance to provide anti-ballistic systems, lead to “horrific casualties and destruction.” He also said that partners unable to help more actively with interceptors could support Ukraine by imposing new sanctions.

Later on Wednesday, Zelensky said he had spoken to NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte about securing more interceptor missiles. In a post on X, he said they had coordinated efforts with countries that have the necessary missiles and the capacity to help. The plea came against the backdrop of what Ukraine describes as a critical shortage of US-made interceptors for Patriots, the system relied on to stop certain ballistic missiles.

The air-defense gap has become central to how the war is being felt in the capital. Ukraine can still intercept most drones, but the missile attacks that slip through are the ones causing the deadliest damage to buildings, warehouses and transport links in and around Kyiv.

What remains unresolved

The broad outline of the overnight attack is clear: deaths in Kyiv and the surrounding region, dozens of wounded, heavy damage to civilian infrastructure, and another Ukrainian appeal for more interceptor missiles. What remains contested is the framing of the targets. Zelensky said the main targets were warehouses belonging to civilian enterprises, while Moscow said it hit logistics and distribution centers tied to weapons, military cargo and drone production.

For now, the immediate response is still centered on rescue work and on trying to close the air-defense gap before the next barrage. Ukrainian rescue crews were still working under dangerous conditions as authorities warned that further strikes could follow.

What happened

China rolled out a series of countermeasures on Wednesday after recent U.S. restrictive measures, widening the dispute to imported office equipment, drone exports, six U.S. entities and certification bodies, while Beijing and analysts framed the moves as part of a broader exchange that could deepen if the U.S. keeps tightening pressure.

A Ministry of Commerce spokesperson said the U.S. measures taken by the Federal Communications Commission and the Department of Homeland Security “seriously violated” the important consensus reached by the two heads of state and infringed on China’s legitimate rights and interests, leaving China “no choice but to respond.” The spokesperson also said China hoped the U.S. would revoke the relevant measures and return to “friendly consultation and cooperation.”

Office equipment probe and drone export curbs

Among the steps, the ministry launched what it called China’s first national security investigation into foreign trade, targeting imported office equipment with printing and copying functions that features foreign system software. Preliminary information, the ministry said, indicates that such equipment may affect national security interests in foreign trade.

The ministry said foreign system software refers to driver software and embedded software developed, tested or maintained by foreign individuals or entities. The investigation will examine the products under probe, their impact on national security interests, domestic demand and reliance on foreign goods, technologies or services, the state and capacity of relevant domestic industries, and the impact of foreign government policies and other matters affecting national security interests in foreign trade.

China also said exports of drones, key components and related technologies on its dual-use export control list will face rigorous case-by-case review when destined for the United States, and will not be eligible for license facilitation measures.

Sanctions on U.S. entities and certification bodies

Beijing also added Compliance Testing LLC to its countermeasure list, saying the company assisted and supported FCC actions that endanger China’s sovereignty, security and development interests. It separately imposed countermeasures on six other U.S. entities: Applied DNA Sciences, Stratum Reservoir, Altana Technologies, Responsible Business Alliance, Verite Group and Human Rights in China.

Under the ministry’s decision, organizations and individuals within China are prohibited from engaging in transactions, cooperation or other related activities with Compliance Testing LLC or any of the six entities.

Separately, China’s National Certification and Accreditation Administration said it would suspend the authorization of U.S.-based certification bodies to conduct follow-up factory inspections for products certified under the China Compulsory Certification system, effective immediately. The CCC system is part of China’s market access regime and is used, among other things, to safeguard national security and prevent market fraud.

A widening dispute and Beijing’s warning

The ministry said the FCC had repeatedly stretched the concept of national security and introduced restrictive measures against China covering telecom operations, testing laboratories, drones, consumer routers, submarine cables and other fields. It also said the U.S. Department of Homeland Security added more than 40 Chinese entities to the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act Entity List on July 31, one day after a video call between the Chinese and U.S. lead persons for economic and trade affairs.

In that setting, analysts said China’s latest export controls and sanctions against American companies underscore Beijing’s expanding economic toolkit and its willingness to use it. Wendy Cutler, senior vice-president at the Asia Society Policy Institute, said the multiple actions China undertook “underscore the breadth and depth of China’s retaliatory toolbox.”

The spokesperson said China’s countermeasures were generally restrained and that China cherishes the stability in bilateral trade and economic ties. But the warning was explicit: if the United States keeps introducing new restrictive measures against China, Beijing said it will take further countermeasures in response.

What happened

Iran said it has agreed the coordinates of a new shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz with Oman, but it also warned that the waterway would not necessarily be safe for all vessels while US pressure and a naval blockade remain in place.

A route agreed in principle

Iran’s foreign ministry said on Wednesday that talks with Oman over future transit routes through the strait had been “moving forward” for the past two months, with the aim of “determining a safe transit route for commercial shipping through the strait”. Foreign ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei said the “geographical coordinates” of the route proposed by the two sides had been agreed, and that a joint statement was in the final stage of review and drafting if “certain third parties” did not obstruct the process.

Kazem Gharibabadi, Iran’s deputy foreign minister for legal and international affairs, said an understanding had been reached on almost all issues, including the location of entry and exit routes for maritime traffic. He said large parts of the route would pass through Iran’s territorial waters and some parts through Oman’s waters. He also said the existing temporary routes would be closed, and that the new route would be used for two to four months or potentially longer.

The ministry did not give specific details on the proposed route. But the broad outline points to a deal that would redirect shipping through a planned corridor rather than reopening the strait in a simple return to previous conditions.

The wider standoff over Hormuz

The talks are taking place against the backdrop of Iran’s near-total closure of the Strait of Hormuz after Tehran and Washington called an end to a June 17 memorandum of understanding and resumed strikes in the war that began when the US and Israel attacked Iran on February 28. Al Jazeera said resumed strikes have disrupted about 20 percent of the world’s oil and liquefied natural gas exports, leading to price increases.

The strait has become the central point of dispute over shipping and control. US officials, including President Donald Trump, have said Tehran is keen for a deal to reopen the waterway, while US officials have repeatedly said they would not accept any arrangement that left Iran in control. Baghaei said the closure of the waterway was due to “US military aggression and the Zionist regime’s aggression against Iran”, and stressed that any understanding with Oman by itself could not mean the strait had become secure for transiting vessels.

He said the factors making the strait insecure “still exist on the part of the United States”, especially “the naval blockade and other aggressive and threatening actions against Iran and its interests”. In the same account, a Tehran-based researcher at the Center for Strategic Studies, Ali Akbar Dareini, said Iran was aiming to “stabilise” its control over the strait, which he called its “biggest military achievement of the war”. He said the negotiations were aimed at “stabilising those achievements”.

What remains unsettled

Even with the route agreement described by Iranian officials, the end point is not clear. Baghaei said any joint statement would only follow if outside parties did not interfere, and he warned that a deal with Oman would not, on its own, make the strait safe for every vessel.

That leaves the wider political question unresolved: whether a route arrangement with Oman can move shipping forward while the dispute with the United States over control, blockade and security remains in place.

Russian families are confronting claims that some women marry recruits just before deployment to secure military death payouts, a practice officials and state media call “black widows,” while Maria says her father’s new widow became his legal next of kin just days before he was killed.

Maria said the Russian military told her that her 59-year-old father, Yury, had been shot on the front lines of the Ukraine war and later died of his wounds. She said she had not known he had enlisted at all, and was stunned again when she was informed that he had married just two days before being sent to fight.

Sitting at the kitchen table in her small St. Petersburg apartment, Maria said, “I’m still in shock and still can’t accept what happened.” She added that it first felt like a dream, and that she sometimes still woke up thinking she must have imagined it. “But it is really happening,” she said tearfully.

Maria said the bride, whom she had never met or even heard her father mention, was 22 years his junior. Because the woman became Yury’s legal next of kin, Maria said she was entitled to official compensation. “I’ve never even spoken to this woman,” said Maria, 37. “I don’t know what to say to her because I immediately suspected it was a scam.”

The payout system at the center of the scandal

When a Russian soldier is killed in battle, the military pays lump sums to the next of kin starting from 5 million rubles, or about $64,000. The compensation is meant to reassure recruits that if they die, their loved ones will be looked after.

But there are mounting concerns in Russia that the benefit is being exploited by scammers. Russian officials and state media dub them “black widows,” saying they persuade vulnerable men to enter sham marriages before sending them to war, then claim the money if the men are killed and cut out other relatives.

The issue has become especially sensitive as Russians hear more stories of recruits allegedly being married off shortly before they die, or even on their deathbeds. In one widely reported case last year, a nurse working at a military hospital was accused of marrying five soldiers in her care before they succumbed to their injuries in order to collect their death benefits; she was fired.

A wider backlash around war, money and marriage

The allegations have touched a nerve in a country where the war in Ukraine, which the Kremlin still calls a “special military operation,” is increasingly felt at home. The pressure comes amid a worsening economy, fuel shortages, the growing human cost of the war, and damaging Ukrainian strikes on Russian soil.

Criticism of the supposed scammers has grown louder, and there have been public calls for an official crackdown. Leonid Slutsky, a prominent lawmaker in Russia’s usually uncritical parliament, said the families of “Special Military Operation” participants need protection from “brazen criminals,” including so-called “black widows,” financial scammers and telephone predators targeting people who receive payouts. He said such crimes were reaching “serious proportions.”

At the same time, after Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, the Kremlin encouraged Russian recruits to get married, a push that could strengthen family support and fit Vladimir Putin’s promotion of “traditional values” amid demographic decline. The authorities also tried to speed up marriages for newly enlisted soldiers by avoiding the usual notice period, and state television showed mass wedding events arranged by local officials.

Disputed marriages, overturned claims and an open investigation.

Some of the most public cases have ended in conflict over who should receive the money. One involved Georgy Kostyrko, a soldier from Ryazan who met Angelina Varyukhina online, married her 10 days later and then rejoined his unit. According to Kostyrko’s mother, Varyukhina later posted photos and videos of herself with other men online, and her son filed for divorce. He was killed before the paperwork was finalized, leaving Varyukhina as the legal recipient of the compensation.

Varyukhina denied the accusations, saying they were meant to “blacken” her reputation and that she and Georgy had reconciled after a falling out. She said the images were posted to make him jealous. Her next-of-kin status was eventually overturned in court, but the case still caused widespread outrage.

There have also been reports of security-force involvement in alleged “black widow” scams. Russian media reported an investigation in Primorsky Krai, in the far east, into a scheme allegedly involving a warrant officer, a sergeant and a military accountant. They were accused of recruiting vulnerable men, including orphans and people with no close relatives, arranging marriages for them and then sending them to high-risk front-line positions where they were more likely to be killed. There have been no public updates on that case.

Back in St. Petersburg, Maria said she took CNN to her father’s old apartment, which has now been sold. She said she believes he was also scammed out of his home. The new owners insisted it was a legitimate sale and said they had never met the seller. Maria said that after the money and the widow are now gone, “There is absolutely nothing left, and that is incredibly painful.”

What happened

MerchantBench introduces a 365-day e-commerce benchmark for LLM agents, testing whether they can keep coherent seller-side decisions over time as delayed order outcomes and supplier events force them to revisit earlier choices.

The preprint frames long-term coherence as the ability to preserve purposeful behavior across extended horizons while adapting to accumulated evidence. It argues that most existing benchmarks focus on bounded tasks with immediate success criteria, while real deployments need agents that can keep operating when feedback arrives late and one action affects later options.

Why seller-side e-commerce was chosen

The authors say seller-side e-commerce is a useful setting because it involves recurring, interdependent decisions in four areas: Product Sourcing, Listing and Pricing Control, Cash-Flow Management, and Mixed-Latency Feedback Adaptation. In that environment, agents face promptly observable Upstream Supplier Events alongside delayed Downstream Order Outcomes, so they have to follow individual order lifecycles and revisit earlier decisions.

MerchantBench is built as a 365-day order-level simulation grounded in 98,843 real e-commerce product records and equipped with 26 tools for agent interaction. The environment is grounded in data from 1688 and covers 365 days from June 1, 2025 through May 31, 2026. It includes product and supplier attributes, daily demand histories, and platform quality and fulfillment signals, with 10 first-level product categories selected for the catalog.

The simulation follows a single-item drop shipping model in which merchants hold no inventory in advance. It models Normal Fulfillment together with six abnormal Downstream Order Outcomes: Cancellation, Stockout, Late Shipment, Returnless Refund, Return and Refund, and Bad Review. The merchant interface is partially observable, exposing current public and realized operational evidence while keeping future demand, event hazards, and presampled outcomes hidden until they become observable.

How the benchmark was run

MerchantBench advances hourly over the 365-day horizon, but the agent receives a decision window only once every 12 steps. The objective is terminal net assets, combining cash balance, security deposit, funds in transit, and receivables. The authors say this setup makes the environment persistent: actions constrain later choices, and incoherent behavior can produce cumulative effects.

To evaluate the benchmark, the paper runs eight LLMs under two agent frameworks, ReAct and Hermes, for 48 runs spanning 365 simulated days. The evaluated models are GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Opus 4.8, Qwen3.7-Max, Qwen3.7-Plus, GLM-5.2, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, and Kimi K2.6. The comparison also includes a rule-based baseline and three human participants without prior e-commerce operating experience.

Each run begins with RMB 2,000 in cash, a RMB 1,000 security deposit, and capacity for 50 active listings. The paper says Hermes adds built-in code execution, planning, memory, and skill management on top of the same 26 MerchantBench tools used by ReAct.

What the results showed

Across the runs, the authors report a substantial gap between the latest LLMs and human participants. The best LLM configuration reached only 27.3% of the mean final net assets achieved by the human participants. In the table of results, GPT-5.6 Sol records the highest final net assets under ReAct, while Qwen3.7-Max ranks first under Hermes; Qwen3.7-Max with Hermes achieves the highest final net assets among all 16 configurations.

The paper also says Hermes outperforms ReAct on average across the eight models, producing 53.3% higher final net assets, 71.5% higher GMV, and 71.2% more orders. But the gains are uneven: Kimi K2.6 is the one model that does slightly worse under Hermes than under ReAct. The authors also note variation in stability, with some configurations showing much larger run-to-run swings than others.

Their trace analysis says two forms of long-term coherence failure help explain the performance gap. One is Operational Coherence, where some agents progressively reduce store intervention and stop following up on earlier decisions or delayed outcomes. The other is Strategic Coherence, where agents lose consistency in sourcing, pricing, or inventory decisions over the year.

In the examples the paper gives, some agents adjust after bad outcomes by replacing risky listings or lowering prices on proven products, while others fail to revise affected listings after refunds or stop filling vacant slots too early. The authors also point to shifting monthly sourcing performance and store activity as evidence that long-horizon business operation remains difficult for the models they tested.

The code is available at https://github.com/KhanCold/merchantbench.
