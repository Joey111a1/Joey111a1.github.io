Brazil: BPC avoided 571 thousand deaths and 13 million hospitalizations in 30 years --> Each percentage point increase in BPC coverage reduced hospitalizations by 2.9%, deaths by 0.6%, says the preprint version of a study

What happened

A preliminary, not-yet-peer-reviewed study from the Barcelona Institute for Global Health (ISGlobal) estimates that Brazil’s Continuous Cash Benefit (BPC) prevented 571,000 deaths and 13 million hospitalizations in the Unified Health System over 30 years. The government benefit, created in 1996, provides a monthly minimum-wage allowance to low-income people aged over 65 and people with disabilities. The study’s authors report that each percentage-point increase in BPC coverage was associated with 2.9% fewer hospitalizations, 0.6% fewer deaths and 3.9% fewer disability-adjusted life years lost.

The research, signed by 13 scientists including Daniella Medeiros Cavalcanti of the Federal University of Rio Grande do Norte and the Federal University of Bahia, also estimates a 5% reduction in mortality among children aged zero to four for each percentage-point increase in BPC beneficiaries. It attributes this effect to grandparents’ caregiving role in poorer households and reports broader health improvements among children in beneficiary families. The study further estimates that each Brazilian real invested in the program avoided R$3.81 in government spending. About 6.6 million people currently receive BPC, according to Brazil’s Ministry of Development and Social Assistance.

Brazil’s health regulator Anvisa has approved Emrelis (telisotuzumabe vedotina) for adults with non-squamous non-small-cell lung cancer that is locally advanced or metastatic, has wild-type EGFR and high c-Met expression, and has progressed after systemic treatment. Anvisa said the approval relied mainly on a study of previously treated patients. China has unveiled revised rules for outbound investment that will replace its 2017 framework from December 2026, expanding the stated role of domestic enterprises, individuals and other organizations while strengthening reporting intended to help investors respond to overseas risks.

China’s Economy Has a Huge Weakness
china_domesticeconomy_and_businessmajor supplement
What happened

China’s manufacturing surge is masking worsening profitability at home: more Chinese industrial firms are losing money, while state support keeps uncompetitive companies operating. The resulting debt, weak demand and dependence on overseas markets expose China—and its trading partners—to mounting economic and trade pressure.

Capital Economics estimates that 30% of Chinese industrial firms are loss-making this year, double the share in 2018. Profits at 5,400 companies listed on Chinese stock exchanges fell for a third consecutive year in 2025, as excess capacity, weak domestic demand and price wars squeezed margins.

Local governments and state-controlled banks continue to support troubled businesses through loan rollovers, tax breaks, subsidies and bailouts, partly to preserve jobs and meet economic targets. The OECD found that Chinese industrial firms received as much as eight times more state assistance than firms in other major economies between 2005 and 2024. The source argues that this support keeps capital tied up in unproductive companies and has helped push nonfinancial-sector debt above 300% of GDP.

With Chinese consumers constrained by an unstable job market and falling property prices, manufacturers are seeking growth abroad. Yet exports bring new risks: Chinese automakers sold nearly 80% more cars overseas in August than a year earlier, while their profits fell 16%. Tariffs and other trade barriers could further strain companies already reliant on state backing and foreign demand.

Spectral-Spatial Interpretation

What happened

A preprint from researchers at the University of Wisconsin–Madison introduces a method for explaining multispectral image-model predictions by identifying which channel matters at each location, rather than producing only a spatial heatmap or a global band ranking.

The method, called Spectral-Spatial Interpretation (SSI), addresses a problem in models that process images with multiple meaningful channels. In RGB photographs, different colors may support predictions in different parts of an object; in satellite imagery, visible, near-infrared and short-wave infrared bands may carry different information about vegetation, water or burned areas. A single heatmap can show where a model relies on evidence but not which channel provides it, while a global channel score can hide local differences.

The work is presented in an arXiv preprint dated 15 October 2026 by Haotian Ma, Ruqi Yang and Philip Townsend, whose affiliations include the University of Wisconsin–Madison’s departments of Computer Sciences and Forest and Wildlife Ecology. The paper has not been peer reviewed.

Allocating evidence within each image location

SSI divides an image into spatial grid cells and treats every location-channel pair as part of the explanation. For one location, it holds the surrounding image at its observed values, then evaluates the channels at that location as a smaller cooperative game. Shapley-value allocation is used to estimate how much each channel contributes to the model’s target-class output in that local context.

The resulting output is a position-by-channel attribution matrix. It can be viewed directly to see joint spatial and spectral evidence, or aggregated in two ways: across channels to obtain a spatial map, and across positions to obtain a channel ranking. The researchers report that the spatial sum at each location exactly matches the model-output contrast produced when that entire location is replaced by the selected baseline.

This is different from an Owen-style hierarchical explanation, which averages contributions across possible surrounding spatial coalitions. SSI instead asks how channels contribute within the particular surrounding image that was observed. The paper says this gives the method a local interpretation, but it also means that the method is designed for position-wise contextual explanations rather than a globally additive accounting of the entire prediction.

The approach also tests whether a simpler factorized explanation is adequate. Such an explanation would multiply one spatial heatmap by one global channel profile, assuming the same spectral pattern applies everywhere. SSI is intended for cases where that assumption fails—for example, where different regions of a bird or satellite scene depend on different channels.

Reported results across three datasets

The authors evaluate SSI on CUB-200-2011 bird images, EuroSAT 13-band Sentinel-2 land-cover imagery and HLS Burn Scar six-band imagery. The models include a ResNet-18 classifier for CUB-200, a ResNet-18 trained on all EuroSAT bands, and a lightweight UNet for burn-scar segmentation. The evaluations use both per-channel mean and zero masking baselines.

Across the three datasets, the preprint reports that SSI achieved the strongest combined deletion and insertion results among the compared dense, flat and rank-1 attribution methods. In these tests, lower deletion scores indicate that an attribution ranking removes target evidence earlier, while higher insertion scores indicate that it restores evidence more effectively.

For the per-channel mean baseline, SSI’s reported insertion AUC was 0.7132 on CUB-200, 0.7319 on EuroSAT and 0.6638 on HLS Burn Scar. The paper also reports leading performance on spectral hit rates and channel-deletion tests for EuroSAT and HLS Burn Scar, as well as the strongest spatial localization on the two datasets with annotations for that evaluation.

On HLS Burn Scar, SSI was compared with an MC-Owen estimator. The researchers report better results for SSI on joint deletion, rank-1 deletion, channel deletion, insertion, spatial localization and spectral hit rates, while using about 6% of MC-Owen’s evaluation budget. In the reported comparison, SSI used up to 4,480 evaluations, compared with as many as 76,800 for the MC-Owen setup.

The paper’s qualitative examples are consistent with the intended use of the method: different RGB channels receive different spatial supports in bird images, while red and short-wave infrared evidence is localized around burn-scar regions. On EuroSAT, the reported explanations emphasize red-edge and near-infrared channels for vegetation classes.

Scope and stated next steps

The experiments use relatively small channel counts: three for CUB-200, 13 for EuroSAT and six for HLS Burn Scar. SSI enumerates or samples channel subsets within each spatial cell, making the local game smaller than a game over every position-channel combination simultaneously. The authors report that their sampling errors remained below 10⁻² in the tested EuroSAT and HLS settings.

The preprint identifies a remaining challenge for hyperspectral imagery with hundreds of correlated bands. It suggests that channel screening or adaptive grouping may be needed before applying local Shapley allocation at that scale. Extensions to operational hyperspectral missions and other multi-channel imaging fields, including biomedical microscopy and industrial materials inspection, are presented as future work rather than demonstrated results.
