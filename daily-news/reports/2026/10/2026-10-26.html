Philippines: What schools will do amid intense heat during El Niño
global_south_and_exploitedpolitics_and_powermaterial_harmregional expansion
What happened

The Philippines’ Department of Education is preparing schools for projected unusually high temperatures as El Niño intensifies, with officials warning that heat-index conditions could increase lost school days and disrupt students’ learning. In an October 23 interview, Undersecretary Malcolm Garma said existing class-suspension rules provide continuity measures while formal El Niño guidelines remain pending. Under the system, face-to-face classes continue at Level 1 with water breaks and adjusted outdoor activities; Level 2 allows modified schedules and home-based or hybrid learning; and Level 3 can be used when heat risks or water shortages become serious. At 42°C or higher, Level 4 suspends academic activities and shifts attention to safety and psychosocial support.

The education department plans to preposition self-learning materials, activity sheets and other alternative-learning resources before the hottest period. Schools are also being directed to maintain drinking-water facilities, address leaks, prepare for rationing, and equip clinics for dehydration or heat stroke, with local governments and the Department of Health available for emergency support. Feeding programs are to develop contingency menus and maintain shelf-stable food stocks. Schools may buy additional fans, while future classrooms are expected to incorporate higher or insulated ceilings and improved airflow. School heads may recommend localized suspensions, but these decisions must be assessed with division offices or local governments.

politics_and_powereconomy_and_businessregional expansion
What happened

Brazil’s incoming Lula administration is preparing a constitutional amendment that could allow deeper budget restraint if revenues fall short, while considering reduced subsidized lending and a larger primary surplus. The proposals are intended to reassure financial markets but could affect public spending, credit access and campaign commitments.

President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva’s team is drafting a PEC, or constitutional amendment proposal, to let the new government pursue the center of its fiscal target and freeze additional budget resources if needed to improve the public-debt trajectory. Lula was reelected on Sunday the 25th, but the amendment has not been formally submitted: that will depend on his decision and on the composition of the new government, particularly the officials leading Finance, Planning and the Civil Affairs Ministry.

The economic team is also considering raising the primary surplus above the R$18.6 billion included in the proposed 2027 budget. The surplus measures revenue minus expenditure before debt-service costs, but the team has not disclosed how it would achieve a higher result. Other measures under consideration include reviewing mandatory spending, submitting rates for new consumption taxes created by tax reform, and ending salary supplements for public servants, especially in the judiciary.

A central part of the plan is to slow subsidized credit. The government may avoid renewing programs such as Move Brasil, which finances vehicles for app-based drivers and taxi drivers and motorcycles for delivery workers. The source reports that subsidized lending expanded during Lula’s third term and has been criticized by specialists; such transfers are classified as financial expenses and therefore fall outside the spending-cap and primary-result rules. More credit could also complicate the Central Bank’s efforts to control inflation.

The proposals must be reconciled with campaign promises including debt relief for microentrepreneurs, a R$5,000 payment for individual microbusiness owners, a higher Bolsa Família benefit, free urban transport and purchases of individuals’ old debts. An economic-team member said the Executive would fit these commitments into the 2027 budget while seeking a somewhat larger surplus.

The rules currently allow a tolerance of 0.25 percentage points of GDP around the annual fiscal target. The proposed amendment would make it easier to contain spending beyond the usual floor if revenue disappoints. Whether Lula adopts the broader package—including a possible change to Health and Education spending floors—remains undecided.

What happened

Researchers at the University of Utah have linked reports of vivid visions of tiny, moving people in China and the Philippines to the mushroom *Lanmaoa asiatica*, identifying a possible biological cause while leaving its active chemical unknown.

The study, published in *Mycologia*, examined a phenomenon reported for decades after people ate wild mushrooms. Those experiencing it describe dozens or hundreds of miniature figures, often brightly dressed, that seem to obey ordinary physical rules: moving around objects, crossing surfaces and interacting with their surroundings. The visions are known as Lilliputian hallucinations.

Researchers previously suspected that similar accounts from widely separated communities might reflect shared cultural expectations. The new investigation instead connected the Chinese mushroom known as “Jian shou qing” and the Philippine mushroom called “Sedesdem” to the same species. Colin Domnauer and Bryn Dentinger interviewed residents, collected specimens in Yunnan and the northern Philippines, and used DNA sequencing to establish their identities and evolutionary relationships.

Chemical and genetic analyses found no known psychoactive compounds, including psilocybin. The researchers therefore suspect that *L. asiatica* produces an unidentified hallucinogenic substance, but that compound has not yet been isolated or characterized. The mushroom’s effects remain a research claim rather than a fully explained mechanism.

The evidence does not yet establish the identity of the mushroom involved in the original Papua New Guinea reports: unusually dry conditions prevented the team from collecting comparison samples there. Even so, the researchers say the matching accounts in three regions support a shared chemical and neurological explanation.

The project also analyzed 53 *Lanmaoa* specimens, identifying 17 species, including four previously unknown to science. Isolating the suspected compound is the team’s next objective, with potential implications for studying how the brain produces detailed visual experiences.
