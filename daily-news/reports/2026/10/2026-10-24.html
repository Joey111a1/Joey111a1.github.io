What happened

The UN reports worsening humanitarian pressure across Yemen, Ethiopia, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Ukraine and Haiti, where conflict, disease and insecurity are displacing civilians, damaging essential infrastructure and stretching relief operations beyond available resources.

In Yemen, more than 256,000 people have been displaced since August, OCHA says. Shelter is the most urgent need for newly displaced families, and more than 2,000 families across five governorates lack it. In Ta’iz city, shortages of fuel, oxygen, medicines and other supplies are putting 42 health facilities at risk, including services for emergency surgery, dialysis, intensive care, and mothers and newborns. In south-eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo, national authorities report more than 55,000 suspected cholera cases this year. Tanganyika has recorded over 6,300, overwhelming treatment capacity in Kalemie and affecting displacement sites.

In Ukraine, attacks reportedly killed at least nine civilians and injured nearly 100, while damage to energy infrastructure left 500,000 people without electricity.

India’s Special Intensive Revision has left millions of names off draft voter rolls, prompting Muslims and other affected voters to fear disenfranchisement, while the Election Commission says the exercise is intended to remove dead, duplicate and otherwise ineligible entries.

What happened

Sudan’s El Fasher has lost more than 90 percent of its population since last year, with about 70,000 people remaining from more than one million displaced residents. Amnesty International says those still there face killings, detention, torture, surveillance and severe restrictions on humanitarian access.

The decline comes one year after the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) captured the North Darfur city. Amnesty said the RSF’s campaign involved crimes against humanity and ethnic cleansing, and that fear now shapes daily life in El Fasher.

To document conditions, Amnesty interviewed 25 civilians over two months: 19 residents and six humanitarian workers. Seventeen of the residents had spent time in RSF detention centres, while 15 had left the city between July and August 2026. Survivors described beatings, arbitrary accusations, ransom demands and prolonged detention. One man said he was held for about four months in a school with roughly 300 other people after returning to collect belongings; another said his family paid to secure his release.

Amnesty said detention sites commonly held women, children, elderly people and wounded people, and that survivors reported widespread malnutrition. A Zaghawa woman also described repeated beatings and ethnic slurs directed at her community.

Some humanitarian organisations had resumed operations by September 2026, but residents told Amnesty that the RSF monitored their movements and prevented private conversations with civilians. The organisation said it contacted the RSF about its findings on 9 October but received no response.

Amnesty called for the release of arbitrarily detained people, an end to torture, unlawful killings and forced labour, and an immediate ceasefire. It also urged an international force to protect civilians and called on countries to halt arms transfers to all parties, particularly transfers to the United Arab Emirates that it says supply the RSF.

china_domesticpolitics_and_powermaterial_harmregional expansion
What happened

China’s Yangtze River restoration is reshaping former industrial waterfronts, with reported gains in water quality and aquatic habitats. The work has also generated approaches to cross-provincial coordination and proposed wider cooperation on river data, flood risks and fisheries affecting communities around the Lancang-Mekong basin.

In Yichang, Hubei Province, a former cargo port has been redeveloped as Lighthouse Square. Structures once used for conveyor equipment now support swings, while an old industrial building serves as an art gallery. Chen Yuanyuan, a resident who patrols the riverbank as a volunteer, recalled that heavy dust once kept people indoors. The site is now used for recreation and environmental education.

The local project, begun in 2018, removed 15 docks, moved 35 enterprises and 113 households, replanted 87 hectares and repaired eight kilometres of river edge. It is part of broader basin efforts to curb pollution, improve water conditions and restore aquatic environments.

The Ministry of Ecology and Environment judged basin water quality excellent in the first quarter of 2026. Across the Yangtze River Economic Belt, the share of monitored locations reaching Grade I to III standards increased from 67 percent in 2015 to 96.5 percent. In April 2025, researchers also observed Yangtze sturgeon spawning and hatching at a rehabilitated site on the Chishui River, after 20 adult fish were placed in a habitat covering about 8,000 square metres. The result was presented as progress toward rebuilding a wild population, while the source noted that full ecosystem recovery will take considerably longer.

A compensation scheme involving Yunnan, Guizhou and Sichuan ties funding to water-quality performance in the Chishui basin. Its initial pooled contribution was 200 million yuan; a later agreement set annual funding at 300 million yuan for 2021-2025. In Yunnan, the account reported Grade II water at an exit monitoring point for five successive years and an increase in recorded fish species from 36 to 43.

World Bank specialist Li Xiaokai said basin governance requires coordination across agencies and administrative borders. China has supplied hydrological data to the Mekong River Commission since 2020 and proposed broader information exchange and joint forecasting. Yunnan University researcher Sun Jingrui said fisheries measures must protect incomes and food security, alongside habitat restoration and targeted restrictions.

Toward Recursive Self-Improvement in Physical AI
science_cs_aiglobal_south_and_exploitedpolitics_and_powertechnology_and_aieconomy_and_businessscience anchorpreprint_not_peer_reviewed
What happened

A preprint by researchers from universities in Singapore, mainland China and Hong Kong proposes a framework for assessing whether robots can improve not only their task performance but also the procedures that generate future improvements, while confronting costly trials, imperfect sensing and irreversible physical damage.

The paper, posted on arXiv on 21 October 2026, examines “recursive self-improvement” (RSI) in physical AI. In this setting, a robot would not merely learn to grasp objects more reliably or navigate better. Its experience would also change how it gathers data, evaluates outcomes or updates its learning system—and an improved version of that process would then help produce further improvements.

The authors distinguish this goal from ordinary adaptation. A robot can become better at a task, or a fixed training procedure can repeatedly produce better versions of a policy, without the improvement process itself becoming more capable. They argue that genuine recursion requires two additional links: knowledge gained from earlier repairs must improve the mechanism that designs future repairs, and that improved mechanism must help generate its own next improvement.

Why physical robots change the problem

Digital agents can rewrite code and test candidate versions in relatively cheap, repeatable and reversible environments. Physical systems face more demanding conditions. Their assessments may rely on sensors, human judgments or learned detectors that miss important parts of an outcome. A mistaken success signal can therefore reinforce a faulty action and send it back to hardware.

Every candidate also consumes robot time, resets, supervision and mechanical wear. Objects can be damaged, calibration can drift and grippers can deteriorate; restoring software does not necessarily restore the physical conditions. Tasks, bodies and interfaces can change as well, so a useful improvement may not survive a new object, a replacement component or a different operating environment.

The paper uses a repeatedly failing grasp as an example. The robot’s policy may lack the correct recovery movement; its world model may misunderstand contact; a memory or program may fail to preserve a successful recovery; or the learning procedure may keep collecting or judging the wrong experiences. The first three cases improve what the robot does. Changing how it gathers evidence, evaluates failures or updates itself is closer to physical RSI.

What the authors find in existing work

The authors place their analysis within robot learning and review systems that improve policies, world models, memories, skills and software procedures connected to physical action. They identify evidence for several lower levels of improvement, especially in manipulation: robots can execute tasks better, retained data or skills can help later learning, and parts of the training procedure can be revised.

Examples discussed in the preprint include systems that learn from deployment corrections, refit models using contact data, retain recovery programs, rewrite reward code in simulation or allow coding agents to modify robot-training infrastructure. Other work lets an adapted policy contribute data to a later generalist model or changes a planner using its own executions. These systems show ways for experience to affect future learning machinery, not only immediate behavior.

However, the authors say the physical studies they examine have not isolated gains specifically caused by recursion. Some systems alter the improver’s knowledge or tools, but that does not by itself show that the improver has become better at improving itself. The paper therefore treats labels such as “self-evolving” or “recursive” as insufficient without comparisons that identify which component changed and what benefit followed.

Conditions and tests for recursion

The proposed framework asks whether four conditions allow improvements to carry forward. Feedback must reveal the remaining problem rather than repeat information the system already has. The receiving component must be able to use the change. The benefit must remain effective when tasks, policies or hardware shift. Finally, further practice must remain safe and affordable.

The authors propose matched experiments using new failures and controlled comparisons. One test would compare an improver before and after it gained knowledge from earlier repairs. A second would examine whether the improved improver produces a successor that outperforms both its parent and a version revised without access to the newly acquired knowledge. These controls are intended to separate a genuine recursive contribution from ordinary procedure improvement or from a fixed system repeatedly applying the same recipe.

The preprint presents these tests as evaluation principles and research directions rather than as evidence that physical RSI has already been demonstrated. Its central conclusion is that robots may improve behavior, learning procedures and eventually the mechanisms that revise those procedures, but that limited trials, unreliable outcome checks, shifting hardware and safety constraints make the recursive claim substantially harder to establish.

Further pretraining can cause models to forget existing capabilities, while retrieval-augmented generation may increase inference latency. Other parametric-memory methods avoid online search but require expensive token-level retrieval to build their training data and typically combine memory only at the output stage.

DFM instead predicts memory at the level of text chunks. A causal predictor uses preceding context to generate several continuous memory vectors, trained against embeddings retrieved offline from a corpus. The same predicted memory is then made available to tokens in the following chunk. A gated cross-attention mechanism inserts that information into selected intermediate decoder layers, allowing later layers to combine it with the model’s evolving context. The backbone remains frozen while the memory predictor and fusion components are trained jointly. During inference, the system generates memory from the input sequence and does not require a datastore search, retrieved passages, or an external embedding encoder.

The researchers report substantially lower supervision-construction costs than approaches based on token-level targets: 32 times fewer retrieval queries and 2,048 times fewer retrieved items. On six knowledge-intensive question-answering tasks, DFM’s average score exceeded that of the strongest baseline by 12.71% with Mistral-7B-v0.3 and 11.19% with Qwen3-8B-Base. The evaluation covered single-hop and multi-hop questions, including NQ, WebQA, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MuSiQue, and Bamboogle.

The reported gains extended beyond question answering. In adaptation experiments covering code, mathematics, and law, DFM produced average improvements of 6.51 points over Qwen3-4B-Base and 4.38 points over Qwen3-8B-Base. The study also reports that fusion placement and gate initialization affected results, while tested chunk sizes and memory-slot counts produced comparatively small changes in average QA performance.

The work was posted to arXiv on 18 October 2026 as a preprint and has not undergone peer review.

A preprint from researchers at Zhejiang University, the University of Hong Kong, the Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, and Shenzhen Loop Area Institute proposes GAFU, a post-training method that uses visual information from a model’s image-generation pathway to improve fine-grained understanding of objects, attributes, counts, and spatial relations.

The work addresses a gap in unified multimodal models (UMMs), which combine visual understanding and generation but have not systematically used their native generation-side representations to support understanding. The researchers argue that conventional understanding pathways can discard local visual evidence before language processing, while generation pathways retain richer detail and spatial structure.

To test this, the team compared the two kinds of representations in six UMMs using 2,084 minimally modified image pairs covering objects, attributes, counting, and relations. Across all 24 model-domain combinations, generation-side representations were more sensitive to subtle semantic changes. They also detected task-relevant changes that understanding-side representations missed. The advantage was reduced when image blocks were spatially shuffled, indicating that preserving location and layout was important. It was strongest for changes requiring local detail or spatial grounding and weaker for counting, which depends more on aggregating instances.

GAFU compresses generation-side features into compact tokens while retaining their spatial organization. It then allows the understanding pathway to retrieve task-relevant information from those tokens. The framework uses reconstruction, spatial-binding, and relation-alignment objectives, followed by preference learning intended to encourage use of relevant generation-side evidence without discarding existing understanding capabilities.

In experiments, GAFU outperformed same-data supervised fine-tuning baselines in all 15 model-benchmark combinations, with average gains of 3.56 to 5.98 percentage points across five benchmarks. The reported maximum relative improvements were 20.00% on VStar-Bench, 17.94% on OmniSpatial, and 8.62% on VSR-Bench. The authors present these results as evidence that generation and understanding pathways can contribute complementary visual information rather than operating as isolated capabilities.

The study is an arXiv preprint dated 14 October 2026 and has not been peer reviewed.

Rebecca Williams of the University of Glasgow recommends investigating disability services, graduate-student representatives, a director of graduate studies and any available financial assistance. What happened

A Rutgers study identifies a possible reason peri-implantitis often withstands antibiotics: bacteria may corrode titanium implants, releasing particles that disrupt immune cells, intensify inflammation and damage the jawbone supporting the implant.

Researchers at the Rutgers School of Dental Medicine examined human tissue samples, cultured human immune cells and genetically engineered mice. Their findings, published in *PNAS Nexus*, suggest that the implant material itself—not only the bacteria around it—can help drive the disease.

Dental implants replace missing teeth for tens of millions of people, but the study’s source estimates that 10% to 20% of recipients eventually develop peri-implantitis, an infection that damages surrounding bone. Antibiotics and professional cleaning are often effective against infections around natural teeth, yet the same approach succeeds in fewer than half of implant-related cases, according to the report.

The researchers found that bacterial biofilms can create acidic conditions that gradually corrode titanium surfaces. The resulting particles, some smaller than a red blood cell, can enter nearby tissue. Particles may also be loosened when implants are cleaned with instruments designed for natural teeth.

Once in the gums, the titanium fragments can attract lipopolysaccharide, a toxin associated with bacteria. Macrophages—immune cells that normally engulf and destroy microbes—attempt to consume the particle-and-toxin mixture but cannot digest the titanium. The cells then remain highly inflamed, releasing signaling molecules such as interleukin-1 beta while becoming less capable of clearing the infection. In laboratory tests, macrophages exposed to titanium particles engulfed fewer than half as many bacteria as untreated cells.

A possible treatment target

The team linked this response to TRPC1, a calcium channel in macrophages. Genetically engineered mice lacking the channel developed smaller abscesses, produced fewer inflammatory signals and cleared bacteria more effectively after exposure to titanium particles and bacteria. The result points to a possible treatment strategy aimed at the immune response rather than antibiotics alone.

The researchers are evaluating drugs that act on this pathway in human cells, but the findings do not yet constitute an available therapy. In the meantime, the study reinforces the importance of professional implant cleaning with nonabrasive methods. The Rutgers researchers say metal scalers used on implants can damage titanium surfaces, potentially releasing more particles and worsening the conditions that contribute to disease.

The reported remission rate is higher than the maximum 10.9% cited in the source for medication outcomes in a 2020 review. The MeRT results therefore remain an early clinical claim from Wave Neuroscience while the detailed findings are prepared for release.

PTSD care has commonly involved psychotherapy and medications such as the selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors sertraline and fluoxetine. Israel Liberzon, the study’s lead psychiatrist and PTSD specialist, said the clearance would add a nonmedication treatment specifically for PTSD.
