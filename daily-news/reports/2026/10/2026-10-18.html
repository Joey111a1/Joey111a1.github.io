What happened

China’s new rules for takeaway and express-delivery packaging are changing how food and parcels are packed, with observed reductions in wrapping, tape and box size. The measures aim to cut waste while preserving leak prevention and delivery safety, but some food items still require extra materials.

Since September 1, Beijing has implemented the country’s first local standard specifically for green packaging by food-delivery merchants. It follows a national mandatory standard on excessive packaging for express deliveries, implemented on July 1. Together, the measures move beyond broad calls for reduction by setting operational requirements for merchants and couriers.

At a Beijing university, students reported that takeaway orders no longer arrived wrapped repeatedly in plastic film and could be opened more easily. The Beijing guidance separates packaging into simple and sealed categories: pastries should be bagged, hamburgers and sandwiches should use fewer layers, rice dishes should use lidded containers without sealing designs, and barbecue items may be wrapped directly in aluminum foil. Foods prone to leakage, including soups and spicy hotpot, still require varied solutions such as sealed containers or separate soup bags. A tea-shop owner said toppings may also need separate packaging to preserve taste.

The express-delivery standard addresses whether boxes fit their contents, the number of packaging layers and tape consumption. At a station in Langfang, Hebei, couriers were using thinner tape and standardized sealing patterns instead of multiple layers. One station supervisor said a roll of the new degradable tape could handle about 40 parcels, compared with 20 using ordinary tape. At Zhejiang University, more than 90% of parcels sent by staff and students reportedly use recycled boxes.

The source reports that by June, standardized packaging covered 86% of the industry, looped packaging accounted for more than 10% of local deliveries, and green-certified materials exceeded 5%. Reused or recycled boxes had been used 1.616 billion times, with reported carbon reductions of 209,400 tonnes. Experts cited in the article say reducing unnecessary packaging shifts waste management toward source reduction and requires coordination among merchants, platforms, delivery companies and consumers.

Initial poses are created through reversible sequences of 30-degree rotations.
