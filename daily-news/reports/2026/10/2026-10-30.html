Researchers have introduced RealSSA, a benchmark for streaming audio-visual target speaker extraction in noisy, reverberant environments, alongside Falcon, a causal model designed for low-latency use on edge hardware. The authors report that Falcon matches or improves extraction quality while using substantially fewer parameters, operations and GPU latency than larger causal systems.

The work, by Wendi Sang, Kai Li, Yifan Li, Jianqiang Huang and Xiaolin Hu, was posted to arXiv on 23 October 2026 as a preprint. Audio-visual target speaker extraction uses a target speaker’s visual stream—typically mouth movement—together with a mixed audio signal to recover that person’s speech. The intended applications include latency-sensitive settings such as video conferencing and hearing-assistive devices.

A benchmark built around difficult acoustic scenes

The authors argue that common benchmarks do not adequately represent deployment conditions. Many use mixtures containing two near-field speakers, with controlled signal-to-noise conditions and limited variation in room acoustics or background noise. They generally do not test changing speaker counts, far-field interference, reverberation, sudden noise events or performance on independently recorded real scenes.

RealSSA is intended to cover those gaps through two components. RealSSA-Sim is a controllable simulated suite spanning small meeting rooms, medium indoor spaces, large public areas and outdoor environments. It varies room size, reverberation, source distance, environmental noise and sudden noise events together. Samples contain one to five dynamic near-field interfering speakers; scenes with far-field interference add 10 to 15 more far-field speakers.

The simulated set contains 100,826 training samples, 9,133 validation samples and 9,169 test samples, totaling about 153 hours. Target audio-visual data come from LRS3, while interfering speech is drawn from LibriSpeech. Continuous noise comes from WHAM! and sudden noise events from Hive. The audio is single-channel at 16 kHz.

RealSSA-Real is a held-out evaluation set recorded with a single mobile device in everyday indoor, public and outdoor environments. It retains natural background noise, reverberation and uncontrolled interfering speech, and is not used for training or validation. The authors evaluate it using perceptual-quality measures, word error rate and target-speaker similarity.

Falcon uses one causal separator pass

Falcon addresses the model-efficiency problem with a single-pass, multi-scale time-frequency separator. Its audio and visual streams are refined and fused, after which an encoder-decoder processes the combined representation at multiple time-frequency resolutions. Unlike lightweight approaches that repeatedly invoke a compact separator, Falcon performs this multi-scale processing in one encoder-decoder traversal.

The training objective combines waveform reconstruction with an encoder-space speaker contrastive loss. That loss brings the extracted representation closer to the clean target speaker and pushes it away from near-field interferers. It is discarded during inference, so the authors describe its leakage-suppression effect as adding no runtime cost. Far-field interferers are not included among the contrastive negatives in the described training setup.

The system is causal: its temporal operations use current and earlier frames rather than future information. The visual pathway uses mouth-region video, while the audio pathway represents the mixture through short-time spectral features before reconstructing the target waveform.

On RealSSA-Sim, Falcon achieved 7.7 dB SI-SNR improvement and 8.2 dB SDR improvement, compared with 7.6 dB and 8.1 dB for causal AV-TF-GridNet. It also led the reported SI-SNR and SDR results on LRS2-2Mix, LRS3-2Mix and VoxCeleb2-2Mix. On the real-recorded evaluation set, Falcon and Swift-Net-12 shared the highest reported DNSMOS score of 3.276.

Falcon’s reported RealSSA-Sim word error rate was 25.47%, compared with 27.31% for AV-TF-GridNet, and its target-speaker similarity was 43.05. On RealSSA-Real, it produced a 32.74% word error rate and the highest reported target-speaker similarity, 42.23, among the extraction systems listed.

The largest difference was computational. Falcon used 0.5 million parameters and 10.6 billion multiply-accumulate operations, compared with 6.2 million parameters and 213.8 billion operations for AV-TF-GridNet. On the authors’ two-second test input, GPU inference latency was 51.3 milliseconds for Falcon versus 567.8 milliseconds for AV-TF-GridNet. The paper describes this as 91.9% fewer parameters, roughly 20.2 times lower computation and 11.1 times lower GPU latency. Falcon also recorded the lowest listed memory use.

Training on RealSSA-Sim improved reported performance on RealSSA-Real compared with training on VoxCeleb2 for the systems tested, particularly for Falcon’s NISQA and DNSMOS scores. These findings are claims from the arXiv preprint; the work has not been peer reviewed.

OmniShowcase takes four images of a subject from different angles. It reconstructs approximate 3D geometry from those images, then renders the reconstruction as a rotating sequence of surface-normal maps. These maps describe the direction of visible surfaces rather than their color, giving the video model information about how the subject is shaped as viewpoints change.

The normal sequence is supplied alongside the four reference images to a pretrained image-to-video diffusion transformer. A text prompt specifies the desired scene and camera movement. The model therefore receives appearance information from the references, a trajectory-independent geometric guide from the normal sequence, and instructions for the target video. The method does not require each normal-map frame to line up with a particular generated frame; the normal sequence acts as a separate conditioning segment.

The underlying generator is adapted with low-rank tuning while its base weights remain frozen. At inference, the same reconstruction and normal-rendering process is applied to a new subject, with no subject-specific optimization reported as necessary.

The authors also created an automated training-data pipeline. It begins with 3D assets, renders the subject along camera paths, and then uses video inpainting to add realistic backgrounds while leaving the rendered subject fixed. This is intended to preserve a single underlying shape throughout the camera movement, unlike a video generator that must hallucinate every newly exposed surface. Video quality and text alignment are more mixed: OmniShowcase generally remains competitive, but it does not lead every reported quality measure. Removing the geometric condition while retaining the reference images lowers identity and consistency performance on both benchmarks. Reducing the normal turntable from 25 views to nine weakens viewpoint coverage.

The reported results show a substantial gap between producing an acceptable final environment state and collaborating effectively with a user.
