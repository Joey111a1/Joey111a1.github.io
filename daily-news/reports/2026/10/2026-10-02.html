DNC sues Trump administration over last-minute changes to overseas voting form
politics_and_powermaterial_harmanchor
What happened

The Democratic National Committee has sued the Trump administration over emergency changes to the Federal Post Card Application, the form used by overseas citizens to register and request absentee ballots. The dispute centers on a removed option that could affect eligible Americans abroad who have never lived in the United States.

The lawsuit, filed on Thursday in federal court in Washington DC, alleges that the administration unlawfully rushed through the change shortly before overseas ballots were due to be sent. Until recently, the FPCA offered four choices describing an overseas voter’s circumstances. Applicants could identify themselves as serving in the military, living abroad while intending to return to the United States, or living abroad without knowing whether they would return. A fourth option covered US citizens who had never lived in the country.

That category applies to citizens born abroad who may still be eligible to vote in US elections. Thirty-eight states and the District of Columbia allow such citizens to vote in at least some elections, even if they have never resided in the United States.

Pentagon sought emergency revisions

In August, as the deadline for sending overseas ballots approached, the Pentagon submitted two emergency requests to modify the form. One of the proposed changes removed the option for citizens who had never lived in the United States. The Office of Management and Budget quickly approved the requests.

In an August letter to the OMB, Sean O’Keefe, the deputy under-secretary of war for personnel and readiness, said the Pentagon had only recently become aware of the option. He argued that the existing versions of the form could confuse voters about the information they were required to provide under federal law. Because the election was less than 90 days away, he said, the changes could not pass through the normal process and had to be expedited.

DNC lawyers called that explanation “pretextual and implausible”. They said the government had at least 15 years of experience issuing guidance to US citizens born and remaining abroad, reviewing public comments on the FPCA and adjusting the form to serve eligible voters.

The DNC alleges that the accelerated changes violated the Administrative Procedure Act, which requires agencies to consider the effects of rule changes before implementing them. It also says the OMB failed to follow proper procedure by not allowing public comment in the Federal Register. The lawsuit asks a judge to declare that the government violated the law and set aside the changes.

The Pentagon did not return a request for comment.

Advocates warn of confusion for voters

The change does not, according to Susan Dzieduszycka-Suinat, president and CEO of the US Vote Foundation, itself remove the voting rights of eligible citizens abroad. She said the Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act guarantees voting rights to US citizens overseas, including service members and their families, and does not require them to have a plan to return to the United States.

But Dzieduszycka-Suinat said the revised form could affect whether applicants recognize themselves among the available choices. A DNC official who spoke anonymously about the lawsuit said voters would see that the familiar checkbox had disappeared and might find that the remaining options did not describe their circumstances accurately. Applicants must sign the form under penalty of perjury, the official said, which could make them reluctant to select an alternative they did not consider correct.

Experts cited in the case said the revision could confuse or discourage citizens who have never lived in the United States from registering and casting ballots. The number potentially affected is unclear. A 2016 government report estimated that about 11,590 US citizens in this category were eligible to vote, while DNC lawyers believe the number has increased.

The wider overseas electorate is considerably larger: the Federal Voting Assistance Program estimates that 2.2 million US citizens living abroad are eligible to vote. About 11% of eligible overseas citizens voted in 2024. The largest groups live in Canada and the United Kingdom.

Parties clash over “never-resident” voting

The form change comes amid Republican efforts to prevent citizens who have never lived in the United States from having their ballots counted. The Republican National Committee has sued in nine states over policies involving these voters. One North Carolina court agreed with the Republicans, while several other courts rejected their arguments and other lawsuits remain pending.

In August, the RNC said it was leading an effort to close what it called a loophole allowing people who had never lived in a state to vote in its elections. It said the effort was intended to protect residency requirements and prevent “never-resident” voting from spreading nationwide.

DNC chair Ken Martin accused Trump and Republicans of trying to eliminate a way for some Americans abroad to register and request mail ballots. He said the party was defending the voting rights of eligible citizens at home and overseas. The federal case will determine whether the administration’s accelerated procedure and the revised FPCA complied with federal law.
